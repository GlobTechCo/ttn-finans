Global Equity Funds Draw $38.63 Billion Even as AI Stock Rout Shakes Markets

Global equity funds added $38.63 billion in the week to November 4, even as technology shares and AI spending worries unsettled markets.

TTN Research DeskNov 6, 2026 · 19:05 UTC

Global equity funds attracted $38.63 billion in net investment during the week through November 4, their seventh consecutive week of inflows, according to LSEG Lipper data reported by Reuters on Friday. The result was the largest weekly intake since September 23. It also created a striking contrast with the sharp decline in technology stocks that unsettled global markets later in the week.

The headline figure does not mean investors were universally optimistic. Money market funds absorbed a much larger $172.71 billion, their largest weekly inflow since April 2020, and global bond funds attracted $9.91 billion. Taken together, the figures suggest simultaneous demand for stock-market exposure and liquid or comparatively defensive assets, rather than an uncomplicated return to risk-taking.

Where the equity money went

U.S. equity funds attracted $14.81 billion, while European funds received $13.25 billion and Asian funds $8.34 billion. The regional figures should not be mistaken for exact contributions to the global total because published fund classifications and coverage can differ. The central point is that investor demand extended across several major regions rather than concentrating in a single domestic market.

Europe's weekly intake was its biggest since August 12. Reuters linked the region's relative appeal partly to a decline in oil and natural gas prices, which had increased inflation pressure amid the Iran war. Lower energy costs can improve the outlook for household spending and corporate margins, although the effects vary widely by sector and country.

The weekly data also captured a period in which major central banks had kept policy rates unchanged. That can influence investor confidence by reducing uncertainty about near-term borrowing costs. It does not guarantee rate cuts or remove the prospect that inflation will force policymakers to keep policy restrictive.

The important timing qualification is that fund flows were measured through Wednesday, November 4. They should not be presented as a definitive account of how investors responded to Thursday's sharp Nasdaq selloff or Friday's trading. Portfolio subscriptions and market prices reflect different information and may move on different schedules.

Technology demand weakens without disappearing

Net investment into technology-sector funds slowed to $623.61 million, a three-week low. This is a change in the pace of inflows, not proof that all investors withdrew money from technology funds. At the same time, healthcare funds received approximately $1.33 billion and financial-sector funds gained $1.2 billion.

That divergence matters because the recent market debate has focused on the scale and economics of artificial intelligence spending. Chipmakers and cloud providers can report strong customer demand while investors question whether expensive data centers will eventually deliver adequate cash returns. A slowdown in sector-specific fund buying is consistent with greater selectivity, but weekly flows alone cannot prove why individual investors shifted their allocations.

For market analysts, the next question is whether investors are reallocating within equities rather than leaving the asset class. Rising demand for financial and healthcare funds could point toward broader diversification. But a single week cannot establish a durable rotation, particularly during volatile trading conditions.

Cash attracted far more than stocks

The $172.71 billion collected by money market funds stands out as the largest figure in the release. Such funds typically hold short-term debt instruments and are often used by institutional and individual investors to manage liquidity. They are not identical to insured bank deposits and do not carry the same guarantees.

The inflow may include routine treasury management as well as deliberate efforts to reduce portfolio risk. Without a more granular breakdown, it would be misleading to interpret every dollar as a reaction to political events or the technology selloff.

Global bond funds registered $9.91 billion in inflows, with government bond funds receiving $3.07 billion. High-yield, euro-denominated and short-term bond funds also drew subscriptions. Those categories can react differently to inflation, central-bank policy and corporate credit conditions, so the aggregate bond figure should not be treated as a uniform bet on falling interest rates.

The distinction is especially important after stronger U.S. employment data raised new questions about how quickly monetary policy might ease. A robust jobs report can support economic activity while also reinforcing expectations that interest rates will remain higher than previously anticipated. That creates competing forces for equities and bonds.

Commodities and emerging markets add context

Precious-metal funds received $269.79 million, partly reversing the previous week's $1.54 billion of withdrawals. Energy funds recorded a third consecutive week of inflows, totaling $327.31 million. Neither weekly figure is large enough on its own to establish an enduring shift in sentiment.

Emerging-market equity funds collected $1.91 billion, their fifth consecutive weekly gain, while emerging-market bond funds recorded $299.07 million in outflows. This contrast underlines why it is risky to describe emerging markets as a single trade. Equity and debt investors face different sensitivities to currencies, funding costs and country-specific risk.

Reuters said the emerging-market figures reflected a universe of 29,229 funds. Coverage, reporting cutoffs and the classification of fund types matter when comparing this release with figures from other databases.

What the numbers do and do not tell us

Fund-flow data measure subscriptions and redemptions, not market returns. A fund can attract new money while the securities it holds fall in price. Equally, a rising market can coexist with net withdrawals if existing holdings appreciate while investors sell units.

Flows also do not measure the beliefs of every market participant. They cover sampled or reported investment vehicles, while direct stock purchases, derivatives and other accounts may be outside the dataset. The figures are valuable precisely because they show one observable component of positioning, not because they provide a complete map of investor psychology.

Next week's fund reports will be more useful for assessing whether the latest AI-related volatility led to a sustained reduction in equity exposure. Analysts will also monitor inflation releases, bond yields and companies' capital expenditure guidance to judge whether the recent market setback changes financing conditions.

For now, the clearest conclusion is narrower than either a bullish or bearish headline might suggest: investors continued adding substantial money to global equity funds through November 4, while moving an even larger amount into money market funds. That combination points to an investment environment in which exposure to growth and demand for liquidity coexist.
