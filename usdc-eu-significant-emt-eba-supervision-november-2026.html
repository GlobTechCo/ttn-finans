USDC: EBA Classifies USDC as the EU’s First Significant E-Money Token

USDC has become the EU’s first significant e-money token, bringing Circle’s stablecoin under closer EBA and French ACPR oversight.

TTN Research DeskNov 14, 2026

USDC has entered a more demanding stage of European supervision after the European Banking Authority classified the stablecoin as the European Union’s first significant e-money token under the Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation. The decision marks an important regulatory milestone for the stablecoin market and introduces a more direct role for the EBA in overseeing key prudential requirements.

The EBA announced the classification on October 14, 2026, following an assessment under MiCA. The authority said that 45 e-money tokens had been notified in the European Union under the regulation, but USDC was the first to meet the criteria for significant status.

The new supervisory framework took effect on November 12. It does not transfer every aspect of oversight to the EBA. Instead, MiCA establishes a dual-supervision arrangement in which the EBA assumes responsibility for specified prudential areas while France’s Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution, or ACPR, retains responsibility for other supervisory functions.

What significant status changes

MiCA distinguishes between ordinary e-money tokens and those considered significant because of their scale or potential importance to the financial system. The classification is intended to ensure that larger tokens receive a level of supervision proportionate to the risks they may create.

For USDC, the EBA’s direct responsibilities include supervision of own-funds requirements, the reserve of assets, liquidity requirements, and recovery and redemption plans. These areas are central to the ability of a stablecoin issuer to maintain the token’s value and meet redemption requests.

The reserve framework is particularly important. A stablecoin designed to maintain a one-to-one value with a fiat currency depends on the quality, liquidity and management of the assets supporting it. If holders lose confidence in the reserve or the issuer’s ability to process redemptions, demand to exchange tokens for traditional currency could rise rapidly.

ACPR retains important supervisory powers

The change does not mean that France’s ACPR has withdrawn from supervision of the issuer. The EBA said the national authority will continue to oversee governance, qualifying holdings, fit-and-proper assessments, outsourcing and conflicts of interest.

The division of responsibilities reflects the structure established by MiCA for significant e-money tokens. The EBA and the national competent authority are expected to cooperate rather than operate as separate regulators with unrelated responsibilities.

This arrangement matters because stablecoin supervision extends beyond reserve composition and liquidity. Governance, management accountability, operational controls and outsourcing arrangements can all affect the issuer’s ability to meet its obligations.

The transition also gives regulators a framework for coordinating information and supervisory decisions across jurisdictions. USDC is issued in the European Union by Circle Internet Financial Europe SAS and in the United States by Circle Internet Financial, LLC. The EBA had signed a memorandum of understanding with the New York State Department of Financial Services in June 2026 to support cooperation and information exchange on cross-border stablecoin activities.

That agreement does not create a single global regulator for USDC. It provides a channel through which authorities can coordinate where the activities of an issuer and its related entities cross jurisdictional boundaries.

Why the decision matters for the stablecoin market

USDC’s classification is significant beyond the issuer itself. Stablecoins are increasingly used as settlement assets across crypto trading, payments and decentralized finance. Their role can extend from exchange balances and blockchain transfers to institutional payment arrangements.

A token that becomes significant under MiCA faces a more intensive supervisory framework. That may increase compliance responsibilities and require the issuer to maintain systems, reporting and resources that satisfy the relevant authorities.

For market participants, the classification also provides a practical example of how MiCA’s significant-token provisions are being applied. Other issuers will be able to assess the EBA’s decision and the division of responsibilities between European and national authorities when evaluating their own regulatory obligations.

The classification should not be interpreted as a guarantee that USDC is free from operational, market or liquidity risk. Regulatory supervision can improve oversight, but it cannot eliminate every possible source of stress. Stablecoin holders remain exposed to the design of the reserve, the issuer’s operational resilience, banking relationships and the legal arrangements governing redemption.

The EBA’s announcement also comes as European authorities continue to develop their approach to digital-asset markets. The distinction between payment tokens, investment products and other crypto-assets remains important because each category can carry different regulatory obligations.

For background on how stablecoins maintain their intended value and the risks that can cause a loss of confidence, see TTN Finance’s guide to stablecoins and depeg risk. The regulatory development can also be considered alongside the USDC corporate payments pilot involving PNC and Coinbase.

A new supervisory phase for USDC in Europe

The November 12 transition gives the EBA a direct role in several of the most important prudential areas governing USDC in the European Union. The ACPR remains involved in governance and other supervisory matters, creating a shared structure rather than a complete transfer of oversight.

For Circle, the classification brings a more detailed European supervisory relationship. For other stablecoin issuers, it demonstrates that significant status under MiCA can change the level and organization of regulatory scrutiny.

The longer-term effect will depend on how the authorities apply the framework in practice and how issuers adapt their reserve, liquidity and governance arrangements. The immediate change, however, is clear: USDC is now the first significant e-money token in the EU, and its supervision has entered a new phase under MiCA.
