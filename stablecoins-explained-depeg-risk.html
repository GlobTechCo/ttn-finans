Stablecoin Depegs: Reserves, Redemption and Holder Risks

A dollar target does not make every token equivalent to cash. Reserve assets, redemption access and market liquidity determine the risks.

TTN Research DeskJul 31, 2026

Updated November 5, 2026: expanded methodology and worked comparisons.

A stablecoin aims to maintain a reference value, often one US dollar. The target does not make the token equivalent to a dollar in a bank account. Its ability to trade near that value depends on its backing, redemption arrangements, market liquidity and the systems through which holders can move between tokens and money.

The distinction becomes visible during a depeg, when the market price moves away from the target. A small deviation can reflect transaction costs or temporary trading conditions. A persistent discount can indicate deeper doubts about reserves or access to redemption. Price alone does not identify the cause. SBI and Shinhan Card’s yen-payment tests put these redemption and settlement questions into a proposed retail-payment setting.

How three backing models attempt to restore a peg

Stablecoin backing and recovery mechanisms
ModelBacking or mechanismWhat recovery depends on
Fiat-reserve backedIssuer holds cash and financial assetsAssets retain value, cash is accessible, eligible holders can redeem and arbitrage remains workable.
Crypto-collateralizedOn-chain collateral and liquidation rulesCollateral prices, overcollateralization, liquidators, oracles and functioning contracts.
AlgorithmicSupply incentives or conversion involving another token, with design-specific reservesDemand and credible incentives survive stress; confidence and liquidity in the supporting token can fail together.

A depeg may persist when the route from a discounted token to its promised redemption value is blocked, costly or doubtful. Identifying the backing model narrows the diagnosis; it does not rank every token in one model as equally safe. Hybrid designs require examination of each dependency.

Different backing models create different exposures

A reserve-backed token may rely on cash and financial assets held by an issuer. Other designs use crypto collateral, often with additional collateral above the amount issued. Algorithmic arrangements may depend heavily on incentives involving another token. These mechanisms should not be grouped together as though the word stablecoin described one balance sheet.

Circle's disclosures provide an issuer-specific example of reserve reporting for USDC. They do not establish the backing of unrelated tokens. The Federal Reserve's analysis of stablecoin fragility emphasizes that demand for prompt redemption can create stress even when an instrument is designed to maintain a fixed value.

For a hypothetical issuer with 1 billion tokens and $1 billion of reserves at stated value, the initial backing ratio is 100%. If reserve assets lose 2% and no capital absorbs the loss, assets become $980 million. That is $0.98 per token before liquidation expenses or other claims. Equal nominal starting values do not eliminate asset-price risk.

USDC: a dated reserve report and conditional redemption

Circle’s September 2026 reserve report lists $73,956,687,701 of reserve assets against 73,878,861,314 USDC in circulation at September 30, 2026, under its defined criteria. The accompanying Deloitte examination is dated October 26. This is a point-in-time attestation of a reserve assertion, not a full financial-statement audit or proof that every holder can immediately obtain bank dollars.

Circle’s USDC Terms, updated October 15, 2026, apply outside the European Economic Area, including the U.S. They distinguish Circle Mint account holders from other token holders. Direct redemption requires an eligible registered Mint account and is subject to the terms and legal restrictions. The terms state that USDC is not covered by FDIC or SIPC protection. EEA holders are directed to separate documentation; these conditions must not be generalized to every token or jurisdiction.

Liquidity differs from solvency

An issuer can hold assets whose ultimate proceeds cover the tokens yet lack cash immediately available to meet withdrawals. Banking hours, settlement cycles, operational outages or the need to sell securities can affect access. A reserve's quality and its availability are related but separate questions.

Suppose the hypothetical issuer has $100 million of immediately accessible cash and $900 million of securities. Holders request $250 million of redemptions. Meeting the requests requires another $150 million from sales, maturities or financing. This is a liquidity problem even before considering whether the securities have lost value.

If selling the required assets incurs a discount, a liquidity problem can turn into an economic loss. It is therefore useful to distinguish a statement about reserves at a reporting date from evidence about how quickly those reserves can be converted under stress. An attestation also has a defined scope and should not automatically be called a full financial-statement audit.

Redemption supports arbitrage only when accessible

If a token trades at $0.97 and an eligible participant can reliably redeem at $1, buying and redeeming may offer a gross spread. For a hypothetical purchase of 100,000 tokens, the $97,000 purchase cost compares with $100,000 of redemption proceeds. The $3,000 difference precedes every fee, delay and risk.

If total costs are $500, the apparent net spread becomes $2,500. But if redemption is suspended or eligibility is absent, the calculation cannot be executed as assumed. The discount can then persist. Retail holders may depend on an exchange market rather than having the same direct redemption access as an approved institutional customer.

A token can also trade above target when demand exceeds immediately available supply or conversion is constrained. A premium is not a new guaranteed reference price. The route back to parity depends on issuance and arbitrage functioning in both directions.

Extra layers add extra claims

A stablecoin held through a trading platform adds the platform's custody and withdrawal arrangements. A wrapped token or cross-chain representation can add bridge and contract dependencies. Placing a token in a DeFi lending arrangement adds another exposure beyond the underlying peg.

For a hypothetical 10,000-token holding marked at $1, a market discount to $0.90 reduces quoted value to $9,000. A separate lending loss could reduce it further. An advertised yield of 5% does not compensate automatically for either event, and the existence of reserves does not guarantee a particular intermediary's promise.

A checklist for the token and the route to cash

  • Issuer: identify the legal entity and the terms applying in your location.
  • Reserve evidence: record the observation date, publication date, assets, liabilities and the assurance scope.
  • Direct redemption: check account eligibility, minimum size, fees, banking cutoffs and any suspension clauses. Confirm current account-specific terms before relying on them.
  • Network: verify the native token contract and whether the issuer supports redemption on that chain.
  • Bridge: identify who holds the original assets and what permits withdrawal of a wrapped representation.
  • Custodian: establish who controls the keys and how withdrawals work if the platform fails.
  • DeFi exposure: read the protocol’s collateral, oracle, liquidation and withdrawal rules separately.

The token’s reserve claim, a platform’s obligation to return customer assets and a protocol’s smart-contract behavior are three different exposures. A token can retain its peg while a custodian blocks withdrawals. A lending position can lose money while its underlying stablecoin remains at $1. Identify which claim has failed before interpreting a quoted price as evidence about all three.

Evaluate the actual claim

Check the issuer, asset composition, reserve-report date, redemption eligibility, costs and relevant legal terms. Token ownership should not be assumed to carry bank-deposit insurance. Protections depend on the actual structure and applicable rules, rather than a logo or a stable market price yesterday.

Compare the product with bank deposits and money market funds only after identifying those differences. A peg is an intended price relationship. The institutions, assets and contractual access supporting it determine how resilient that relationship may be when holders seek cash at the same time.
