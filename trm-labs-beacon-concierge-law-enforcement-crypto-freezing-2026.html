TRM Labs Launches Beacon Concierge to Help Law Enforcement Freeze Illicit Crypto

TRM Labs introduced a managed tracing and coordination service connecting verified law enforcement agencies with crypto exchanges and stablecoin issuers to freeze illicit assets.

TTN Research Desk Nov 13, 2026 · 4:58 AM EST

Blockchain analytics company TRM Labs has introduced Beacon Concierge, a managed service designed to help law enforcement agencies trace illicit cryptocurrency and coordinate requests to freeze or seize funds. The service connects verified authorities with participating crypto exchanges and stablecoin issuers, aiming to reduce the time and complexity involved in stopping suspicious assets from moving through digital-asset networks.

Announced on November 12, Beacon Concierge is available to verified law enforcement agencies worldwide, including those that do not hold a TRM software licence. The launch addresses a practical problem in crypto investigations: identifying the movement of illicit funds is only one part of the process. Investigators must also establish which platforms can act, prepare the necessary evidence and deliver requests while the assets are still accessible.

TRM says the new service has already supported 15 cases involving 14 agencies across 10 countries, resulting in more than $35 million in frozen funds.

From blockchain tracing to coordinated action

Cryptocurrency transactions are recorded on public blockchains, which can provide investigators with a detailed record of transfers between wallet addresses. However, tracing funds does not automatically give authorities the ability to stop them.

Illicit assets can move rapidly between addresses, blockchains and different digital assets. Criminal networks may split funds into numerous transactions, use cross-chain services or transfer assets through multiple intermediaries. In major incidents, stolen funds can be distributed across thousands of transactions within days, according to TRM Labs.

Even when investigators identify the relevant wallets, they may face difficulties reaching the exchanges or stablecoin issuers that can take action. Agencies may not have established contacts with those companies, or may lack the technical resources needed to prepare requests in the format required by each platform.

Beacon Concierge is intended to bridge that gap. After an agency completes verification and submits a request, TRM's investigators help trace the funds, identify relevant addresses, prepare an evidence package and flag the addresses through the Beacon Network. The company then supports the request through the process of contacting participating platforms and following up on potential freezing or seizure actions.

The service is designed to cover investigations across different blockchains and digital assets through a single coordinated process.

The Beacon Network provides the infrastructure

Beacon Concierge builds on the Beacon Network, a coordination platform launched by TRM Labs in August 2025. The network connects verified law enforcement agencies with cryptocurrency exchanges, stablecoin issuers, payment companies and other relevant organisations.

According to TRM, Beacon has more than 200 members, collectively covering approximately 80% of cryptocurrency trading volume. The company says the average time to action is measured in minutes when flagged funds reach a participating member.

The network's scale is important because the effectiveness of an investigation can depend on whether information reaches the platform holding the assets. A wallet address identified by investigators may not be directly connected to an exchange at the moment it is flagged. However, if funds later move to a participating platform, the alert can help that platform identify the transaction and assess the relevant information.

TRM reports that more than $200 million has been interdicted with Beacon Network members to date. The company also says the Financial Action Task Force referenced the network in its November 2025 asset recovery guidance as an example of a public-private approach to recovering criminal assets.

These figures are company-reported results. They indicate the scale of activity associated with the network, but they should not be interpreted as a guarantee that every flagged transaction will lead to a successful freeze or recovery.

Why stablecoins are central to the process

Stablecoins play an important role in cryptocurrency markets because their value is generally linked to traditional currencies, most commonly the U.S. dollar, with different stablecoin backing and redemption structures shaping how issuers manage reserves and holder obligations. They are widely used for trading, transfers and payments, making them relevant to both legitimate financial activity and investigations involving illicit funds.

Some stablecoin issuers have technical and contractual mechanisms that allow them to freeze tokens at specific blockchain addresses, alongside institutional tokenized payment initiatives operated by regulated financial institutions. This can give investigators an opportunity to prevent funds from being transferred or converted after an address has been identified and the issuer has received a valid request.

However, the ability to freeze assets is not universal across all cryptocurrencies or blockchain networks. Bitcoin and many other digital assets do not have an issuer with the same direct control over individual balances that certain centrally issued stablecoins provide.

A freeze is also not automatic simply because an address has been flagged. The relevant issuer or exchange must assess the request under its own procedures and applicable legal requirements. Beacon Concierge can help prepare and coordinate the process, but it does not independently exercise the legal authority of law enforcement or the control mechanisms of an asset issuer.

This distinction is important for understanding what the service can realistically achieve. Its role is to improve the speed and quality of coordination, rather than to guarantee that every suspected illicit asset can be immobilised.

The Binance exploit illustrates the scale of cross-chain investigations

TRM has pointed to the Beacon Network's work related to the Binance exploit as an example of the complexity involved in following funds across multiple blockchain networks.

According to the company, Beacon has labelled more than 26,000 addresses across 14 blockchains and traced more than 67,000 transfers and links, including over 1,050 cross-chain swaps. The network followed funds from 59 origin addresses through an average of 10 transaction hops, with some paths extending to 71 hops.

TRM also reported that participating network members generated more than 45,000 alerts. These figures illustrate how quickly a single incident can develop into a large investigation involving numerous addresses, transfers and platforms.

For individual investigators, manually following every transaction path can be difficult, particularly when funds are divided and moved across different networks. A coordinated system can help distribute relevant information to organisations that may encounter the assets later in the transaction chain.

What Beacon Concierge changes for law enforcement

The main change introduced by Beacon Concierge is broader access to coordinated support. Previously, an agency's ability to work with exchanges and stablecoin issuers could depend on existing relationships, specialist staff and its access to blockchain analytics tools.

By offering a managed service without requiring a TRM licence, the company is seeking to make that process available to a wider group of verified authorities. TRM's investigators take on much of the technical work involved in tracing funds and preparing requests, while the agency remains responsible for the investigation and the legal basis for its actions.

The model reflects a wider development in digital-asset enforcement: successful investigations increasingly depend on cooperation between public authorities and private companies that operate or support cryptocurrency infrastructure.

Beacon Concierge does not eliminate the legal, technical or operational challenges involved in recovering crypto assets. Its significance lies in bringing tracing, evidence preparation and platform coordination into a single managed process. As illicit funds continue to move across networks and services, the ability to act quickly and communicate effectively with the organisations holding those assets is becoming an increasingly important part of cryptocurrency investigations.
