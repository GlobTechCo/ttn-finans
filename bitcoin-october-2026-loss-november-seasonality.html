Bitcoin Lost 7.3% in October; November’s Historical Average Hides Wide Swings

November’s historical mean return of 34.59% is far above its 10.26% median. A small, uneven sample offers little basis for a dependable forecast.

TTN Research Desk2026-11-01

Bitcoin lost a reported 7.32% in October, while Ether declined 16.06%, according to historical monthly-return figures circulated on November 1. The numbers placed October among the weaker months for both assets. November has often been a stronger month historically, but that record does not establish what prices will do next.

A report distributed through KuCoin cites a 34.59% average November return for Bitcoin across 16 observations since 2010. Ten of those Novembers were positive and six negative. That headline average is striking, but the same figures give a median return of just 10.26%. The gap between the two numbers reveals how much the arithmetic mean can be shaped by unusually large years. The figures are attributed to a third-party report on KuCoin’s news page, rather than to a standalone proprietary study by the exchange.

Why October's weakness matters

The figures also need to be read as end-of-month estimates rather than as a universal reference price. Different data vendors can report slightly different percentages depending on the exchange basket, candle boundary and whether they measure Bitcoin in dollars or a stablecoin. Independent monthly-return tables broadly support a loss of roughly 7.3% for Bitcoin, although their historical aggregates do not always match the 16-year study quoted in KuCoin’s feed.

November's headline average hides extreme outcomes

The same November analysis identifies a gain of 453.6% in 2013 and a loss of 36.41% in 2018. Those outcomes show why a 34.59% historical mean should not be used as a base-case forecast. An extraordinary increase in an earlier, smaller market can pull the average sharply higher even if more typical Novembers have been much less dramatic.

The median of 10.26% describes the middle of that sample, not an expected return for November 2026. Six of the 16 observations were negative, and Bitcoin’s market size, trading venues and investor base changed considerably over the period.

What investors should watch instead

A useful November assessment begins with market behavior rather than a calendar pattern. Spot trading activity can indicate whether buyers are absorbing supply after October’s selloff. Where available, exchange-traded fund flows can offer another view of institutional demand, although a single day of inflows or outflows is not enough to define a durable trend.

Derivatives funding rates, open interest and liquidation concentrations also deserve attention. A rebound driven mainly by leveraged positioning may look impressive but can reverse quickly when prices move against crowded trades. More sustained improvement would ideally involve rising spot demand alongside measured leverage and healthy liquidity.

A cautious reading of the data

The October losses are a substantive market development. The frequently quoted November average is useful as historical context, not as evidence that Bitcoin is due for a sharp rebound. A 34.59% mean, a 10.26% median and six negative Novembers tell a more complicated story than a single seasonal headline suggests.
