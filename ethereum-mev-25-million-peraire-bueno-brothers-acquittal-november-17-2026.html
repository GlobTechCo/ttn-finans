Ethereum MEV Case Ends in Acquittal After Judge Rejects $25 Million Wire Fraud Charges

A U.S. federal judge has acquitted Anton and James Peraire-Bueno of wire fraud and money laundering charges tied to a $25 million Ethereum MEV transaction-ordering operation.

TTN Research DeskNov 17, 2026 · 2:19 PM EET

A U.S. federal judge has acquitted Anton and James Peraire-Bueno, two brothers accused of extracting approximately $25 million from automated trading bots operating on Ethereum. The decision ends a closely watched criminal case that tested how federal wire fraud laws apply to sophisticated trading strategies in decentralized financial markets.

U.S. District Judge Jessica G. L. Clarke granted the brothers' motion for a judgment of acquittal on November 16, ruling that prosecutors had failed to establish the material misrepresentations required to support the wire fraud charges.

The case centered on an operation carried out in April 2023 that allegedly exploited weaknesses in infrastructure used by automated trading bots. Prosecutors argued that the brothers manipulated transaction flows and intercepted bundles submitted by other market participants.

How the alleged Ethereum exploit worked

The operation involved maximal extractable value, or MEV. The term describes value that block producers or other participants can obtain by influencing the ordering, inclusion or exclusion of transactions in a blockchain block.

On Ethereum, MEV activity often involves automated strategies that monitor pending transactions and attempt to profit from price movements or transaction ordering. A sandwich attack, for example, places one transaction before a target trade and another afterward to benefit from the resulting price movement.

Prosecutors alleged that the brothers used bait transactions to attract automated trading bots. The bots submitted bundles containing valuable trades, which the brothers allegedly intercepted through a vulnerability in MEV-Boost relay infrastructure.

MEV-Boost is software used by Ethereum validators to connect with block builders and receive proposed blocks. According to prosecutors, the brothers used a relay vulnerability to access private transaction bundles and replace proposed block contents with their own transactions. The alleged operation extracted approximately $25 million in cryptocurrency within around 12 seconds.

The defense disputed the government's characterization and argued that the strategy involved activity in an unregulated market rather than criminal fraud.

Why the judge rejected the prosecution's case

To secure a wire fraud conviction, prosecutors needed to prove material misrepresentations as part of a scheme to obtain money or property. Judge Clarke concluded that the government had not established the necessary false statements.

The court found that the bait transactions did not promise that they would be included in the bots' bundles or that the brothers would allow themselves to become targets of sandwich attacks. The judge also rejected the argument that submitting an invalid block header with a digital signature constituted a material misrepresentation. Prosecutors had not demonstrated how the allegedly false information influenced a material decision by the relay. The court further found that registering a validator, requesting a block and proposing a modified block did not, on the evidence presented, amount to legally actionable misrepresentations.

A transaction can be contrary to the intended operation of a software system without automatically satisfying the elements of a federal fraud offense. The ruling focused on the specific charges rather than making a broad judgment about the legality of MEV strategies.

A case that lasted more than two years

The brothers were arrested in May 2024 and charged with conspiracy to commit wire fraud, wire fraud and conspiracy to commit money laundering. The case proceeded to trial in October 2025. After nearly four weeks, a Manhattan federal jury failed to reach a unanimous verdict and the judge declared a mistrial on November 7, 2025.

The acquittal removes the remaining charges. The judge also dismissed the money laundering conspiracy charge because it depended on the alleged proceeds of wire fraud. Without the underlying offense, the required predicate criminal activity was not established.

What the decision means for decentralized finance

The case exposed a difficult question for blockchain markets: how should existing financial crime laws apply when trading strategies exploit the technical rules of decentralized systems?

Traditional financial markets have detailed rules governing manipulation, disclosure and trading conduct. Decentralized protocols often rely on open-source software, automated execution and economic incentives rather than a central operator.

The acquittal may be cited by participants who argue that exploiting transaction-ordering opportunities is not automatically fraud. It does not provide blanket protection for market manipulation, theft, unauthorized access or conduct that violates other laws.

For developers and protocol operators, the case highlights the importance of relay security and transaction privacy. MEV infrastructure influences how transactions are ordered and processed, and vulnerabilities can create costs for users and automated trading systems.

Legal clarity remains incomplete

The acquittal resolves the criminal charges against the brothers, but it does not settle every legal question surrounding MEV. Future cases may depend on the specific software, market structure, conduct and legal claims involved.

The immediate result is that prosecutors failed to prove the material misrepresentations necessary for the charges in this case. The broader challenge of defining acceptable conduct in decentralized markets remains unresolved.
