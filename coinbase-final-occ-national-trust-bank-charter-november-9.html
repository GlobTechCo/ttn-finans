Coinbase Wins Final OCC Approval for a National Trust Bank Charter

The federal charter moves Coinbase's institutional custody business under a single national regulator, while a banking-industry lawsuit and Senate criticism keep the legal footing contested.

TTN Research DeskNov 9, 2026 · 12:00 UTC

Coinbase announced on Monday, November 9, that Coinbase National Trust Company had completed its charter process with the U.S. Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC). The regulator's public application database records an approval dated April 2, 2026, and a consummated or effective date of November 6, 2026. The charter is designed for institutional custody and fiduciary services, rather than conventional retail banking, and should not be confused with permission to accept insured retail deposits.

What the charter allows

The trust company can provide fiduciary custody of digital assets, mainly for institutional clients, including holding and transferring assets in custody accounts, according to Crypto News. Staking, prime trading and financing will remain with Coinbase affiliates, and the trust bank would act only as an intermediary for those services. It excludes ordinary retail deposits, traditional lending and direct stablecoin issuance. Coinbase has said it is not becoming a commercial bank and will not take retail deposits or engage in fractional-reserve banking, according to Decrypt.

Trade reporting says Coinbase plans to migrate some custody activity from its New York-regulated entity to the federally chartered structure. The public OCC application record confirms the charter status but does not, by itself, establish the exact timing or financial conditions of that migration. Any reported capital and liquidity requirements should be verified against the final supervisory documents before being presented as definitive.

The charter does not automatically give Coinbase access to Federal Reserve payment systems, which requires a separate process, according to Crypto News.

How it fits with other approvals

Other crypto companies have also sought national trust charters, reflecting demand for federal regulatory treatment of digital-asset custody and related services. Each applicant’s status and permitted activities must be checked individually against OCC records, because a conditional approval is not the same as an effective charter.

The broader charter pipeline includes applicants with different proposed business models, from institutional custody to trust structures associated with stablecoin services. The operational scope and approval stage matter more than the number of companies in the pipeline. Investors should not treat an application or conditional decision as permission to operate every traditional banking activity.

The OCC application history resolves an important chronology question: Coinbase's filing was received October 3, 2025, its application was approved April 2, 2026, and the charter became effective November 6, 2026. These dates refer to different procedural stages, not separate contradictory final approvals.

What Coinbase says

In its November 9 announcement, Coinbase described the charter as part of its effort to offer custody under a consistent federal regulatory framework. Coinbase has said the approval lets it expand custody within a consistent federal framework. American Banker reported that Coinbase plans to move later into stablecoin issuance, servicing, white-label stablecoin arrangements and payments. That plan is a stated intention and requires further steps.

Coinbase has offered regulated custody since 2018, when its New York trust company received a limited-purpose trust charter, according to Decrypt. A single federal regulator would replace a patchwork of state oversight for that business.

Opposition and legal risk

The approvals are contested. The Independent Community Bankers of America sued the OCC on October 2 in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia, according to Crypto News, arguing that crypto trust banks lack safeguards that insured banks have and calling the charters a "side door" into banking, according to Decrypt. Senator Elizabeth Warren has argued that the OCC's crypto charter approvals, including Coinbase's, are illegal, according to Decrypt.

American Banker noted that critics' core concerns are that these firms can operate like banks without traditional bank regulatory requirements and that assets held at trusts are not federally deposit insured. It said litigation may take years and that a court could tighten restrictions on existing charters or reverse the OCC's interpretation.

Why it matters for the market

The charter is a regulatory step and has no direct link to the price of bitcoin or other tokens. The reports reviewed do not describe a move in Coinbase shares, and we cannot report one. For institutional clients such as asset managers and ETF issuers, a federally supervised custodian can simplify compliance reviews, which is the practical significance noted in coverage, though the reports do not quantify any new business.

The approval arrives while broader U.S. crypto legislation remains stalled. Separate legislative debates over market structure are ongoing, and the OCC charter does not resolve the wider statutory rules for cryptocurrency exchanges. The bill is explained in our CLARITY Act primer, and the political backdrop is in our midterm lobbying report.

The market backdrop

Bitcoin traded near $74,560 on Monday after touching its lowest level in more than two months, according to Reuters via MarketScreener, as covered in our bitcoin liquidation report. U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs lost about $1.28 billion last week, according to Blockhead.

What to watch

The first marker is the OCC's approval document and any conditions it contains. The second is whether the Fed grants payment-system access to trust banks. The third is the court schedule in the community bankers' lawsuit. The fourth is how pending applicants such as BitGo, Fidelity Digital Assets and Paxos fare.

Limits of the reporting

The OCC application record and Coinbase's November 9 company announcement confirm the central event and dates. Some operational details cited in trade reporting, including the timing of any migration and specific supervisory conditions, still require confirmation from the final charter documentation.
