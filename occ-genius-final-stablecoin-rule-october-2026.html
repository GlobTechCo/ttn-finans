OCC Finalizes Stablecoin Rules Ahead of January 2027 Compliance Deadline

The OCC’s final GENIUS Act framework takes effect January 18, 2027, alongside licensing guidance and a separate reporting process.

TTN Research Desk2026-10-21

The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency issued its final rule implementing the GENIUS Act on Wednesday, setting a January 18, 2027 effective date for a federal framework covering payment stablecoin issuers under its jurisdiction. Accompanying bulletins address applications, possible waivers and reporting. The package turns a legislative framework into operational requirements that affected issuers must prepare to meet.

The development is a final implementing rule, rather than a new law or an approval of every existing stablecoin. Congress enacted the GENIUS Act in July 2025. The OCC’s October 21 bulletin describes the requirements for entities within its authority, including relevant bank subsidiaries, federally qualified nonbank issuers and certain foreign and state-qualified issuers.

From authorization to ongoing supervision

The OCC says its new 12 CFR Part 15 addresses reserves, redemption, risk management, custody, audits, applications and supervision. It also covers capital and an operational backstop. The framework extends beyond the technical ability to create or transfer a token: the issuer’s financial and operational arrangements become part of regulatory oversight.

The final rule does not complete every related regulatory process. The agency says Bank Secrecy Act, anti-money-laundering and sanctions requirements will be addressed through a separate rulemaking coordinated with the Treasury Department. Readers should therefore distinguish finalization of this package from a claim that all stablecoin-related rulemaking is finished.

For token users, the distinction between a payment instrument and its issuer remains essential. A blockchain can record that a transfer occurred without establishing that the recipient can redeem at the expected value when needed. TTN’s guide to stablecoins and depegging explains how reserve quality, liquidity and redemption arrangements affect that relationship.

An application does not equal approval

Separate OCC guidance warns that submitting an application before the deadline may not itself satisfy the requirement for prior approval. Its waiver process addresses the possibility that an issuer seeking to comply could still be waiting for a decision when the law takes effect. A request for relief and an authorization to continue operating are different steps.

The bulletin describes an initial 30-day review to determine whether an application is substantially complete, with missing information returned to the applicant for correction. The agency commits to acting no later than 120 days after that completeness determination. Those periods make the quality and timing of a filing commercially important, rather than a purely administrative detail.

An issuer planning a launch must coordinate regulatory review with banking relationships, custody, technology and customer commitments. Promising a product before the required authorization is in place can create a mismatch between commercial expectations and what the business is permitted to do. The existence of a waiver mechanism should not be treated as an automatic extension for every applicant.

Reporting has its own approval process

The OCC also published revised weekly and quarterly reporting forms for comment. Bulletin 2026-52 says the collection is intended to support compliance, supervision and transparency about issuers’ financial condition. It requests comments within 30 days of publication in the Federal Register, preserving a separate review step for the reporting arrangements.

Subject to Office of Management and Budget approval, the proposed collection would begin with a January 25, 2027 weekly report date and a March 31, 2027 quarterly report date. These reporting dates are distinct from the January 18 effective date of the law and final rule. Treating all three as one deadline would obscure what the agency actually announced.

What matters for the market next

The business implication is a clearer set of preparations for affected issuers, accompanied by licensing and implementation work. Compliance systems have costs, but a formal framework can also help banks, payment businesses and customers evaluate potential counterparties. Neither effect establishes which issuer will gain market share or whether demand for a particular token will increase.

The next useful evidence will be application decisions, any granted waivers and completion of the reporting process. Users and business partners should look for the permissions and redemption terms that apply to the specific issuer they use. Wednesday’s action advances the regulatory framework; it does not remove the need to assess the actual company, product and arrangements behind an individual stablecoin.
