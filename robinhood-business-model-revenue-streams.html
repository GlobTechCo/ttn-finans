Robinhood’s Business Model: How the Trading App Makes Money

Trading, interest income and subscriptions give Robinhood several growth engines, but each carries different economic and regulatory risks.

TTN Research DeskNov 25, 2026 · 9:52 AM EST

Robinhood Markets has grown beyond its commission-free stock-trading app into brokerage, options, event contracts, crypto, cash products, lending and paid services. The test is whether these activities can produce durable revenue through changing markets and regulation.

A brokerage built around customer activity

Robinhood Markets (NASDAQ: HOOD) operates a digital retail-finance platform. Eligible customers can trade stocks, exchange-traded funds, options, futures, event contracts and cryptocurrencies, as well as use retirement accounts and margin lending. Product availability and protections vary by jurisdiction.

The company reduces visible trading commissions and earns revenue elsewhere in the transaction and customer relationship. “Commission-free” does not mean every service is cost-free: customers may still encounter regulatory fees, spreads, financing charges, subscription costs or other product-specific expenses.

Three revenue categories explain the economics

Robinhood reports transaction-based revenue, net interest revenue and other revenue. In the third quarter of 2026, total net revenue reached $1.424 billion, up 12% year over year. Transaction-based revenue rose 19% to $867 million; net interest revenue fell 9% to $415 million; and other revenue increased 61% to $142 million.

Transaction revenue is generated when customers trade. For US equities and options, Robinhood receives payment for order flow (PFOF) from market makers that execute eligible orders. Crypto transactions have generated rebates from liquidity providers, while the company has also introduced fee-based pricing for some crypto activity. These arrangements make execution quality, spreads and regulation central to the model.

Options generated $399 million, up 31% year over year, while equity transaction revenue rose 41% to $121 million. Cryptocurrency transaction revenue fell 55% to $120 million. Event-contract revenue reached $173 million, compared with $27 million a year earlier. The mix shows diversification, but also how growth in one product can mask weakness in another. Interest income depends on rates and balances

Net interest revenue comes from margin lending, customer cash and sweep balances, securities lending, credit-card balances and corporate cash investments, less relevant interest expenses. It varies with rates, balance composition, borrowing demand and securities-lending conditions.

In the third quarter, margin interest revenue was $244 million and cash-sweep revenue was $45 million. Net securities-lending revenue was negative $19 million, compared with positive $87 million a year earlier. Revenue from these activities can weaken even as margin borrowing grows.

Gold and financial services deepen the relationship

Robinhood Gold adds recurring subscription revenue and bundles features such as higher interest on eligible cash and research, subject to current terms. Gold subscription revenue reached $62 million in the third quarter, up 32% year over year; subscribers totaled approximately 5.18 million. Other revenue growth also reflected service revenue from Trump Accounts.

Retirement, cash management, credit cards and advisory services may improve retention, but bring servicing costs, credit exposure and compliance obligations.

Crypto and event contracts widen the opportunity

Robinhood’s crypto business includes its retail trading service and Bitstamp, the exchange acquired in 2025, which adds international and institutional operations. Bitstamp is distinct from Robinhood Chain: an exchange is a trading venue; a blockchain is network infrastructure.

October operating data show crypto’s cyclicality. Robinhood and Bitstamp recorded $17.0 billion in combined crypto notional trading volume, down 48% year over year. Event contracts traded reached 5.9 billion, up 136%. Volume is not revenue, and the two measures should not be treated as economically equivalent.

Event contracts add growth potential but face legal challenges in parts of the United States. Rules differ by contract, venue and jurisdiction; Robinhood discloses litigation and regulatory uncertainty. Growth faces an earnings and execution test

At the end of October 2026, Robinhood reported 28.9 million funded customers and $383.2 billion in platform assets. October net deposits were $15.1 billion. Yet crypto volume fell 48% year over year, showing that asset gathering and trading activity can move in different directions.

For the third quarter, net income attributable to Robinhood was $468 million, down 16% year over year, while diluted earnings per share fell 16% to $0.51. Sales growth must be weighed against operating costs, credit-loss provisions and non-GAAP adjustments. TTN’s explanation of earnings beats and misses outlines why one headline number rarely tells the whole story.

Robinhood competes with traditional and app-based brokers, including Interactive Brokers and Charles Schwab. Readers comparing providers can start with TTN’s broker overview; fees and protections depend on country and legal entity.

Robinhood has built a more diversified financial platform, but not a risk-free or uniformly recurring revenue stream. Its long-term test is whether subscriptions, customer assets and broader services can make earnings more resilient when trading cools, interest income shifts or regulators challenge a fast-growing product. That is a more useful measure of the business than app downloads or a single quarter’s record revenue.
