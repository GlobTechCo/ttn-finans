Bitcoin Prediction Markets Price a $70,000 Retest as Traders Weigh Year-End Risks

Prices on Polymarket and Kalshi suggest continued uncertainty, but their contracts measure different outcomes and should not be treated as a single forecast.

TTN Research DeskNov 7, 2026 · 19:36 UTC

Bitcoin prediction markets indicated continued downside concern on Saturday, November 7, with contracts on Polymarket placing the implied chance of the cryptocurrency touching $70,000 before the end of 2026 at roughly 51% during a source check. Contracts for touching $90,000 were near 24%. The price of these contracts changes with trading activity, so the figures are a time-specific reading rather than stable odds or a verified prediction.

A separate Kalshi market showed the year-end settlement brackets of $75,000 to $79,999.99 and $70,000 to $74,999.99 carrying some of the largest individual probabilities, near 17% and 15%, respectively. Those percentages should not be added to Polymarket's touch-market numbers: they refer to different questions with different settlement rules. The distinction is central to interpreting the story fairly.

Two platforms, two questions

The Polymarket contract asks whether bitcoin reaches a specified level at any point before the end of the year. A temporary price dip can make a $70,000 touch contract pay even if the market later rebounds. Multiple thresholds can resolve in the affirmative. By contrast, Kalshi's year-end contracts are mutually exclusive ranges based on a final reference price near the end of December.

Kalshi's published rules say settlement uses CF Benchmarks' Bitcoin Real Time Index. Sixty one-second observations in the final minute are averaged for the relevant year-end determination. The method matters because a last trade on one exchange need not match that averaged benchmark. Anyone comparing odds must first know what the contract actually pays for.

A contract price around 51 cents is often expressed as an implied probability of roughly 51%. It is not necessarily the probability a statistically calibrated forecasting model would assign. Fees, bid-ask spreads, liquidity and investor positioning can all influence the price. Prediction-market odds deserve attribution to the trading venue and a timestamp, not treatment as objective probabilities.

The $70,000 scenario

On Polymarket's 2026 touch market, pricing during verification was near 51% for a move down to $70,000, near 29% for $65,000 and near 18% for $60,000. The contract for a move up to $90,000 was near 24%. These figures reflect the market quotes retrieved during this review, not prices guaranteed to remain available at publication.

A stronger headline would avoid declaring that traders definitively forecast a year-end price of $70,000. The lower threshold is an intrayear touch outcome. An investor could simultaneously believe bitcoin will briefly drop to $70,000 and finish the year considerably higher. The same position cannot be inferred from a single contract price.

Kalshi presented a more distributed set of year-end brackets. The $70,000 to $74,999.99 and $75,000 to $79,999.99 outcomes each attracted meaningful trading, but neither represented a majority. Rather than showing consensus around one exact price, the market priced a range of possibilities with substantial uncertainty.

ETF withdrawals are context, not proof

The prediction-market shift followed a difficult week for digital assets. US spot bitcoin and ether ETFs recorded combined net outflows of approximately $1.65 billion during November 2-6, according to reporting using Farside Investors' fund-flow data. Our separate report on bitcoin and ether ETF withdrawals details the institution-level flows.

ETF redemptions can indicate lower demand for fund shares over a defined period. They do not establish the exact price bitcoin must reach next. Likewise, the implied probabilities on a prediction platform do not demonstrate a causal link between redemptions and the eventual direction of the spot market. These are related signals, not interchangeable measurements.

The existing TTN Finance guide on what bitcoin ETFs change about ownership explains the financial structure behind those products. ETF investors typically own fund shares rather than cryptocurrency directly, so fund-level buying and selling is not equivalent to every bitcoin holder liquidating coins.

Why market context matters

Technology shares also fell sharply during the week, and investors reassessed AI-related valuations and the interest-rate outlook. Those conditions can influence the broader appetite for risk assets, including bitcoin, without making cryptocurrency prices move in lockstep with equities. Investors should avoid overinterpreting a short period in which both markets weakened.

A previous TTN Finance report described bitcoin's move near $80,000 during a Fed-heavy week. Today's market-pricing discussion is different: it compares contracts that describe alternative future outcomes instead of predicting an imminent trading breakout.

The useful measure to monitor is not a single dramatic percentage. It is whether liquidity and contract terms remain comparable when the odds change, whether one side of the market is thin, and whether other venues show a similar distribution. Predictions markets provide a window into the positions some traders are willing to take. They do not replace spot-market data, fundamental analysis or the underlying contract rulebook.
