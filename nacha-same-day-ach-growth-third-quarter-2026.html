Same-Day ACH Payments Jump 36.7% as Businesses Move Money Faster

Nacha reported 470.4 million same-day ACH payments in the third quarter, while business-to-business transfers continued to grow.

TTN Research Desk2026-10-20

Nacha reported Tuesday that Same Day ACH handled 470.4 million payments in the third quarter of 2026, up 36.7% from a year earlier. Their value reached $1.3 trillion, compared with $970.4 billion in the same quarter last year.

The figures show faster transfers gaining ground within an established payments network. Across all ACH payments, transaction count rose 5.9% to 9.3 billion, while their combined value increased 10.2% to $25.6 trillion.

Faster transfers outpace the wider network

The contrast is the central finding: same-day transaction growth substantially exceeded the network-wide increase. The two categories should not be added together, because same-day transfers are part of the ACH total.

For a business, a shorter payment cycle can reduce the time between authorizing a transfer and settling an obligation. That matters when a supplier needs funds before releasing goods or a company wants to keep its payment schedule closer to the date an invoice falls due.

Payment speed can also affect the timing of cash moving through a business. It does not by itself create additional profit: a transfer that happens sooner is still a payment the company owes. The benefit depends on how the business manages the resulting cash schedule.

Business payments remain a source of growth

Nacha said business-to-business ACH payments approached 2.3 billion in the quarter, rising 9.7% from a year earlier, as companies continued moving away from checks.

That shift can have operational value beyond speed. Electronic payments can be easier to reconcile when they are connected to invoice records, although a company still needs reliable approval controls and accurate recipient information. Faster movement makes those checks more consequential, rather than removing the need for them.

The payment mix explains more than the dollar headline

Using Nacha's rounded totals, same-day transactions represented roughly 5% of all ACH payments in the quarter. Their share of dollar value was also about 5%. That calculation puts the rapid growth in perspective: faster settlement is expanding strongly from a smaller base, while the broader network still accounts for most payment activity. A 36.7% increase in this subset does not mean the entire network grew by that amount.

The distinction between volume and value is equally useful. Dividing the reported $1.3 trillion by 470.4 million payments gives an average of approximately $2,760 per same-day transaction. Because the published dollar total is rounded, that is only an indicative calculation. It is not a typical consumer bill, a median transfer or evidence that most payments were near that size; large business transfers can pull an average upward.

Nacha describes Same Day ACH as completing debit and credit payments within hours on the same business day. That is different from promising an instantaneous transfer at any hour of the week. A business choosing a payment service needs to check its bank's submission deadlines and availability terms rather than assuming that the product name guarantees immediate receipt in every circumstance.

An urgent payroll correction and a routine supplier invoice illustrate the trade-off. The first can benefit directly from a shorter delivery cycle. The second may already be scheduled comfortably ahead of its due date. Paying extra for speed on every invoice would need a separate justification. Treasury staff should compare the service charge, the consequence of delay and the work needed to reconcile each payment, rather than treating faster processing as an automatic saving.

What the totals do not establish

Network value is the amount transferred, not revenue earned by Nacha or a measure of new economic output. It can rise because there are more payments, larger individual transfers or a different mix of activity.

The report therefore offers evidence of changing payment habits. It does not establish that household spending or corporate sales grew at the same rate. For businesses evaluating faster settlement, the practical question is which payments benefit from a shorter cycle and whether the operational benefit justifies the service cost.
