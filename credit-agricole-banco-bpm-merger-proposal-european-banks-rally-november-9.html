Banco BPM Jumps 10% on Crédit Agricole Merger Proposal as European Banks Lead a Cautious Rebound

A proposed combination of Crédit Agricole's Italian arm with Banco BPM and a strong UniCredit quarter lifted euro-zone banks on Monday, while cheaper oil and a tentative recovery in global stocks set the tone before U.S. inflation data.

TTN Research DeskNov 9, 2026 · 7:00 AM EST

Crédit Agricole’s proposal to combine its Italian banking subsidiary with Banco BPM has moved beyond an initial approach, but it remains a negotiation rather than an agreed transaction. Banco BPM’s board began a formal assessment on November 12, and chief executive Giuseppe Castagna said four days later that the potential deal was not a done deal. For investors, the central question is no longer simply whether the banks could create a larger lender. It is whether the proposed exchange terms adequately compensate Banco BPM shareholders for giving up the bank’s independence.

Crédit Agricole announced on November 9 that it had invited Banco BPM to discuss a merger with Crédit Agricole Italia. The combined group would be built around Banco BPM and remain listed in Milan. Crédit Agricole said it would bring its Italian life and non-life insurance businesses into the new entity, alongside its banking subsidiary. The proposed group would serve about 6.5 million customers through more than 2,300 branches, making it Italy’s third-largest banking group by the companies’ stated estimate.

The proposal would convert Crédit Agricole’s existing 29.6% holding in Banco BPM into an estimated stake of about 45% in the combined company. Banco BPM shareholders would receive shares under an exchange ratio that Crédit Agricole says reflects a 20% premium. That premium is calculated using projected 2027 price-to-earnings multiples for Banco BPM and the businesses contributed by Crédit Agricole; it should not be read as a simple 20% cash uplift to the market price. The French lender has also proposed an extraordinary dividend of €1 billion.

Crédit Agricole estimates that the combination could generate €800 million in annual pre-tax synergies and lift Banco BPM earnings per share by approximately 24% in 2027. Those figures are the bidder’s projections, not independently verified savings or guaranteed shareholder returns. Their delivery would depend on the final transaction structure, integration costs, execution and the performance of the combined bank. The companies have not disclosed a binding merger agreement or a detailed integration plan.

Banco BPM’s board response on November 12 showed why the proposal is not a formality. After reviewing the letter, directors authorized chief executive Giuseppe Castagna to begin discussions with Crédit Agricole and examine whether the transaction is feasible in the interests of stakeholders. At the same time, the board said the combination would substantially reduce the contestability of Banco BPM. It argued that any valuation must reflect both the loss of the bank’s independence and the strengths of its existing business model. The board also initiated the work of its related-party committee.

That concern matters because Banco BPM is not simply a retail deposit franchise. The bank has developed earnings streams in insurance, payments and asset management alongside traditional lending. A merger could provide scale, broader distribution and access to Crédit Agricole’s wider financial-services capabilities, but shareholders must assess what value those businesses contribute to the combined group. The board’s reference to contestability also signals that it wants a valuation that reflects the strategic option value of Banco BPM remaining independent, rather than relying only on the bidder’s synergy assumptions.

Castagna reinforced the cautious message on November 16. Speaking to reporters, he said the situation had been interpreted as if the transaction were already settled, when discussions were still at a very preliminary stage. He said the banks would continue working to determine whether an industrially sound project could also be financially attractive to Banco BPM shareholders. He also said the proposal to retain him as chief executive of the combined group had not been discussed with him and was not necessary for a transaction to proceed. His comments leave open both the possibility of negotiations advancing and the possibility that the parties fail to agree.

The proposal arrives amid a broader consolidation cycle in Italian banking. Crédit Agricole already has a significant shareholding in Banco BPM, while UniCredit’s separate pursuit of Commerzbank has kept cross-border banking strategy in focus. The two situations are not the same transaction, but both highlight how European lenders are weighing scale, capital strength, domestic market positions and regulatory constraints. Our coverage of UniCredit’s Commerzbank strategy examines the wider consolidation backdrop. The commercial logic of the proposal also fits the broader mechanics discussed in our mergers and acquisitions explainer.

Any agreement would face several hurdles. Crédit Agricole’s proposal envisages approval by Banco BPM’s extraordinary shareholders’ meeting, including the voting conditions associated with the proposed “whitewash” procedure, as well as customary regulatory authorizations. The lender has specifically cited European Central Bank approval and Italy’s golden-power regime. The final legal structure has not been settled, and the parties would need to clarify governance, the contribution of the insurance businesses, the exchange ratio and the treatment of minority shareholders before investors could assess a definitive offer.

Investors should therefore distinguish three separate milestones: the opening of discussions, a negotiated and binding transaction, and the approvals required to complete it. Only the first has clearly occurred. The next material evidence will be any revised terms, a formal agreement, details of the valuation methodology and the board’s recommendation to shareholders. Until those appear, the €800 million synergy estimate and 24% earnings-per-share uplift remain Crédit Agricole’s forecasts. Banco BPM’s response makes clear that the value of independence is part of the negotiation, not a footnote.
