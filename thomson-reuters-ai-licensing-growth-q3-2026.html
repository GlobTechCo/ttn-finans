Thomson Reuters Raises Outlook as AI Licensing Grows

AI licensing helped lift Reuters news transaction revenue 56%, while higher technology costs squeezed the legal business’s adjusted EBITDA margin.

TTN Research Desk2026-11-03

Thomson Reuters reported third-quarter revenue of $1.83 billion, up 10%, and raised its full-year organic growth outlook on November 3. AI-related licensing supported news revenue, while higher technology costs reduced the Legal Professionals segment’s adjusted EBITDA margin.

Guidance improves after a stronger quarter

The company now expects 2026 organic revenue growth of 8.5% to 9%, up from an earlier expectation of approximately 8%. Organic growth attempts to isolate performance in the existing business by adjusting for currency and portfolio changes. It is therefore a more useful indicator of underlying customer demand than a simple comparison of reported revenue across periods that include acquisitions and disposals.

Adjusted earnings were 81 cents per share. Organic revenue grew 9% in Legal Professionals and 11% in both Corporates and Tax, Audit & Accounting. Recurring subscriptions remain an important source of revenue across these businesses.

The financial presentation excludes the global print division after the company sold a majority stake. Thomson Reuters announced the completion of the sale of 51% of that business to investors advised by KKR, with gross proceeds of about $500 million. The change matters for interpreting guidance: investors should not assume that every headline growth number is calculated against an unchanged corporate portfolio.

Generative AI becomes a licensing business

One of the most striking figures concerns Reuters itself. News transaction revenue increased 56% to $44 million, supported by licensing activity connected with generative AI. The figure is not the total revenue of the Reuters news division, nor does it mean that all Reuters content is sold to AI developers. It shows that transactions involving news content are becoming a material growth channel at a time when publishers and software firms are negotiating how valuable information can be used in AI services.

The company highlighted enhancements to CoCounsel Legal and CoCounsel Tax, Audit and Accounting as part of its growth story. These products are intended to support professionals whose work depends on accurate, current and properly sourced information. In legal and tax environments, the cost of an incorrect answer can outweigh the convenience of rapid generation. That makes trustworthy data, permissions and human review central to the product design, not incidental marketing features.

TTN Finance has examined related enterprise adoption questions in its analysis of AI agents and business returns and the risks of granting AI systems operational access. Thomson Reuters occupies a particularly interesting position because its customers want both the automation benefits and the accountability of professional work.

Growth comes with a cost

The Legal Professionals segment’s adjusted EBITDA margin fell to 46.6% from 49.0%, reflecting higher technology and other costs. This measure excludes items including depreciation and amortization.

What investors should measure

Thomson Reuters also faces questions about investor expectations. Its shares had fallen 6.3% this year through November 2, compared with a roughly 10% gain in the S&P 500, according to Reuters. Relative share performance is not a direct verdict on product quality, but it indicates that a stronger financial report does not automatically settle valuation questions.
