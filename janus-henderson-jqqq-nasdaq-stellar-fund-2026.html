Nasdaq-100 Fund Launches on Stellar, With U.S. Investors Excluded

Janus Henderson and Centrifuge announced JQQQ, a tokenized index fund. Its legal structure and investor restrictions matter as much as its blockchain.

TTN Research Desk2026-10-27

Janus Henderson and Centrifuge announced a blockchain-native fund tracking the Nasdaq-100 on Tuesday, taking a widely followed stock-market benchmark onto the Stellar network. The product, called JQQQ, is available only to eligible investors in permitted jurisdictions and is not available to U.S. persons, according to the launch disclosures.

That restriction is central to understanding the news. A fund can provide exposure to a U.S. equity benchmark without being a U.S. retail investment product. A public blockchain makes transactions visible and programmable; it does not by itself make every investment accessible to everyone with a wallet. Eligibility and the legal vehicle still determine who may participate.

CoinDesk reported that the launch follows the earlier Janus Henderson Anemoy S&P 500 Fund, SPXA. The new product broadens the firm's onchain index offerings, but it should not be confused with an announcement that an existing U.S.-listed ETF has moved to Stellar. Our explanation of ETF mechanics describes a particular market structure; a blockchain-native fund needs to be assessed on its own terms.

The legal roles are more precise than the headline

The fund is a segregated portfolio of Anemoy Capital SPC Limited, a British Virgin Islands company. Its investment manager is Anemoy Asset Management Limited, with Janus Henderson Investors US LLC serving as sub-investment manager. Centrifuge supplies the onchain infrastructure, while Nasdaq licenses the index. Nasdaq does not issue, endorse or sell the fund, the disclosures state.

These roles separate the investment exposure from the systems used to operate and distribute it. Index licensing identifies the benchmark that a strategy seeks to follow; it does not transfer responsibility for the fund's operations to the index company. Nor does a recognizable benchmark name make the token equivalent to holding each underlying company's shares directly.

The launch materials expressly describe indirect economic exposure and say the product does not convey shareholder rights with respect to the index. For readers, the practical question is which legal claim the fund share represents, who administers it and how that claim is exercised. The blockchain record is one part of that arrangement, rather than a replacement for the surrounding documents.

Moving fund shares is different from trading the holdings

Issuing shares on a network can make it easier to connect a fund with software-based financial services. But the transfer of a token and the valuation, trading or settlement of underlying investments are distinct processes. A continuously operating network does not automatically create continuous liquidity at the fund's fair value.

Our guide to decentralized finance explains why a transaction executed by software still relies on rules and outside inputs. For an index fund, a useful assessment would examine dealing arrangements, valuation, costs and the ability to meet redemptions. Tuesday's announcement is evidence of a product launch, not an independent demonstration that those features outperform conventional alternatives.

The companies also describe potential collateral, lending and other onchain uses. Their disclosures qualify those possibilities: third parties would provide such functionality, it may not be available to every investor or jurisdiction, and it falls outside Janus Henderson's management of the fund. Reporting it as an already universal borrowing service would go beyond what has been announced.

The important test is usable distribution

The Stellar launch adds another venue for tokenized investment products. It does not establish a particular increase in demand for the network's native token, and the announcement does not disclose JQQQ's opening assets. The more than $1 billion cited for Janus Henderson's onchain platform covers multiple strategies; it is not the amount raised by this new fund.

Our coverage of a tokenized sterling fund class highlights the same distinction between a new distribution format and the investment underneath it. JQQQ's significance is the extension of an established equity benchmark into that format. Its next tests will be actual investor adoption, reliable operation and clarity about how eligible holders can use and redeem their shares. None of those outcomes can be inferred from the presence of a token alone.
