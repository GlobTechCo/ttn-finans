Software Outage Fears Add to Wall Street Sell-Off as Technology Shares Slide

U.S. technology, software and cybersecurity equities declined on November 17 as investors weighed recent infrastructure outages, sticky inflation data and leadership uncertainty ahead of Nvidia's quarterly earnings.

TTN Research DeskNov 17, 2026 · 8:21 PM EET

Market Analysis · November 17, 2026 · 8:21 PM EET

US stocks fell on November 17 as investors weighed concerns about software reliability, a reported outage affecting the NSA’s classified internal communications network and uncertainty ahead of Nvidia’s quarterly results. The decline extended a broader technology-led selloff already under way, rather than establishing the outage as a single cause of the market move.

The S&P 500, Nasdaq Composite and Dow Jones Industrial Average all moved lower during the session. The selling followed losses in technology and semiconductor shares on the previous day and reflected overlapping concerns about high valuations, borrowing costs and the outlook for AI-related spending.

A report that the National Security Agency’s internal classified communications network was offline added to those concerns. CNN reported the outage, citing people familiar with the matter. Reuters said there was no immediate evidence of a cyberattack and that the full scope and duration of the disruption were not known.

Software shares were among the session’s weakest groups. Barron’s reported steep declines in Salesforce, ServiceNow and Intuit as the selloff broadened beyond semiconductor stocks. The losses show investor sensitivity to software valuations and business spending, but they do not establish that the NSA outage caused the moves.

A series of failures changes the risk discussion

The NSA report arrived after several other technology incidents in the United States. JPMorgan Chase said a software bug in its on-premise hardware systems had delayed some transactions on November 16. The bank said there was no evidence of a cyberattack and that the issue was not systemic.

United Airlines also experienced a system outage that delayed departing flights. Earlier, a malfunction affecting the Federal Aviation Administration’s air-traffic-control communications had disrupted US air travel. Microsoft experienced a major service interruption the previous week.

These incidents have not been linked by investigators, and their reported causes differ. However, their concentration has drawn attention to the operational dependence of large institutions on complex technology systems.

For investors, the issue is not limited to the direct cost of a single outage. A failure can interrupt customer transactions, delay services, create regulatory questions and require expensive remediation. Companies that sell software and infrastructure to essential industries may face additional scrutiny if customers begin demanding stronger guarantees of availability and recovery.

The financial impact can also extend to suppliers. Businesses may postpone new contracts, increase spending on backup systems or diversify vendors. That could create opportunities for some technology providers while putting pressure on companies whose products are associated with outages or whose customers are already reviewing technology budgets.

Nvidia remains the central test for AI valuations

The broader decline is unfolding before Nvidia’s quarterly results, scheduled for release after the US market closes. Nvidia’s results are a key test of demand for AI infrastructure.

Investors will focus on revenue, margins, supply conditions and management’s outlook for demand from cloud providers and other large customers. The earnings call will also be closely watched because interim chief executive Colette Kress is leading the company following Jensen Huang’s departure.

Huang said on November 17 that he would be willing to return if the board decided that doing so would serve the company’s interests. The statement added uncertainty to an already sensitive leadership transition. Nvidia’s board has not announced that it intends to reinstate him.

The combination of leadership questions, export restrictions and a volatile market has increased the importance of the company’s guidance. Strong results could support the view that AI infrastructure spending remains resilient. However, investors may still react negatively if management signals slower growth, higher costs or uncertainty about access to important markets.

The wider market is also sensitive to the possibility that AI-related investment has become too concentrated. Large technology companies have committed substantial capital to data centres, processors and power infrastructure. If expectations for future returns weaken, the effects could spread across suppliers, software businesses and financial markets.

Interest rates complicate the outlook

Technology shares are also facing pressure from interest-rate expectations. Higher borrowing costs can reduce the present value investors assign to future earnings, making expensive growth stocks particularly sensitive to changes in bond yields.

October retail sales rose 0.2%, the Census Bureau reported, while sales excluding motor vehicles increased 0.6%. The agency said the headline estimate’s margin of error included zero. Separately, import prices rose 1.1%, above the 0.7% Reuters poll forecast, reinforcing concerns about cost pressures.

That combination complicates the outlook for monetary policy. Stronger economic activity can support corporate revenue, but persistent inflation may give the Federal Reserve less room to reduce interest rates. Higher rates can weigh on valuations even when current business conditions remain stable.

What investors will watch next

The next major catalyst is Nvidia’s earnings report and management commentary. Investors will look for evidence that demand remains strong enough to justify the sector’s investment plans and valuations. They will also watch whether the recent leadership disruption has affected customer confidence or the company’s ability to execute.
