Bitcoin ETFs Lose $1.44 Billion as Outflows Extend to Five Days

U.S. spot Bitcoin funds recorded a fifth consecutive session of net withdrawals on November 11, with BlackRock's IBIT accounting for most of the latest daily outflow.

TTN Research Desk November 12, 2026

U.S. spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds recorded five consecutive trading sessions of net outflows through November 11, extending a period of withdrawals that has put institutional demand for the products under renewed scrutiny. The funds lost approximately $1.44 billion over the five sessions, including about $299.8 million on Wednesday, according to ETF flow data tracked by market sources.

The latest figures show that withdrawals were concentrated in a small number of products rather than distributed evenly across the market. BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust, known by its ticker IBIT, accounted for $269.7 million of the November 11 outflow. Grayscale's GBTC recorded another $39.1 million in withdrawals, while Morgan Stanley's MSBT attracted approximately $9 million.

The pattern matters because spot Bitcoin ETFs have become an important channel through which U.S. investors gain exposure to the cryptocurrency through traditional brokerage and investment accounts. Their daily creations and redemptions provide a visible, though incomplete, measure of demand from investors using regulated fund structures.

Five sessions of withdrawals

The five-day period from November 5 through November 11 produced approximately $1.44 billion in net outflows. The withdrawals added to a difficult start to November for the products, following Bitcoin's fifth consecutive losing week. Net outflows for the month reached approximately $1.88 billion through November 11, following roughly $520 million in withdrawals during October.

The daily figures were not identical. November 5 saw withdrawals of approximately $430 million, followed by about $412 million on November 6. The funds then recorded approximately $201 million in outflows on November 9 and $95 million on November 10, before another $299.8 million left the products on November 11.

The sequence suggests that selling pressure persisted across several sessions rather than appearing as a single isolated redemption event. It does not, however, establish that every investor was making the same decision or that all withdrawals reflected a change in long-term expectations for Bitcoin. Fund flows can reflect a range of portfolio decisions, including risk reduction, profit-taking, changes in asset allocation, tax considerations and transfers between investment vehicles.

IBIT accounts for most of the latest decline

BlackRock's IBIT was the largest contributor to the November 11 outflow. Its $269.7 million withdrawal represented roughly 90% of the day's net selling across U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs.

Grayscale's GBTC contributed another $39.1 million in outflows. Morgan Stanley's MSBT moved in the opposite direction, attracting approximately $9 million in net inflows. Most of the other funds recorded no material net flow for the session.

The concentration is important when interpreting the market-wide total. A large withdrawal from one major product can materially influence the aggregate figure even when many competing funds record little activity. IBIT remains one of the most significant products in the U.S. spot Bitcoin ETF market, having accumulated substantial net inflows since its launch, while GBTC has experienced persistent cumulative withdrawals as investors have reassessed the structure and costs of the converted trust.

Assets under management have declined

Total net assets across U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs stood at approximately $93.7 billion in the latest reported data. That is substantially below the peak of roughly $169.5 billion recorded in October 2025.

The difference reflects both changes in Bitcoin's market price and the net movement of capital into and out of the funds. Assets under management are not a direct measure of investor withdrawals: a decline in the underlying asset price can reduce fund assets even when investors do not redeem shares.

Cumulative net inflows since the products began trading in January 2024 remained around $55.2 billion. That figure provides useful context for the recent selling. Cumulative net inflows measure the balance of creations and redemptions over time, while net assets reflect the current market value of the holdings.

Broader market conditions

The withdrawals coincided with pressure across technology and other risk-sensitive assets. That overlap is consistent with a period of reduced risk appetite, but it does not prove that equity-market weakness directly caused the ETF withdrawals. Investors may have been responding to broader portfolio constraints, changing expectations for interest rates, volatility in digital assets or other factors.

The correlation also does not establish that ETF redemptions alone drove Bitcoin's price performance. Spot prices are influenced by activity across global exchanges, derivatives markets, liquidity conditions and investor positioning, as demonstrated during the recent Bitcoin selloff and exchange liquidation event.

What the outflows do — and do not — show

Five consecutive days of withdrawals are a clear sign that demand through the ETF channel weakened during the period. The concentration of the latest selling in IBIT also shows how much the aggregate market figure can depend on activity in the largest funds.

Still, the data do not demonstrate that institutional investors have abandoned Bitcoin or that the long-term adoption case has reversed. Cumulative net inflows remain positive, and the funds continue to hold tens of billions of dollars in assets. The next sessions will help determine whether the withdrawals represent a short-term adjustment or a more persistent change in allocation.
