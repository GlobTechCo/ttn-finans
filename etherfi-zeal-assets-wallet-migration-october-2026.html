Crypto app EtherFi buys Zeal assets as wallet users face a December move

Zeal asks users to move assets by December 1. Migration to EtherFi is optional, and the deal does not transfer customer accounts, keys or funds.

TTN Research Desk2026-10-24

Crypto-finance app ether.fi has acquired selected assets of Zeal, with the wallet app preparing to wind down and asking users to move their holdings by December 1. The acquisition, announced October 23, does not automatically transfer customers' accounts, private keys or money. Users can choose the offered migration or move their assets elsewhere.

The distinction changes the practical meaning of the deal. This is a purchase of selected business assets accompanied by an invitation to users, rather than an automatic relocation of their balances. For Zeal customers, the immediate issue is continuity: how to move holdings and payment functions before the app closes, while understanding the terms of whichever service they choose next.

December 1 is the requested move date

Zeal says a final date for the app to stop working has not yet been set. Its request to move assets by December 1 should therefore be distinguished from a confirmed shutdown at a specific hour that day. The company says it will publish the closing date in the app and on its website.

That distinction is useful for customers planning around payments or transfers. A requested migration deadline supplies a planning point; an app's final operating date determines when its interface becomes unavailable. Neither should be replaced by an assumption that every feature will continue indefinitely. The notice gives users a reason to review their arrangements before the wind-down progresses further.

The acquisition also illustrates a limit to the idea that self-custody removes every dependency. Control of an asset and continued access through a particular app are separate matters. Our explanation of how decentralized-finance services fit together describes why the wallet interface, networks and applications can each create operational dependencies even when a user retains control over a transaction.

The offered migration creates a separate account

Ether.fi's migration page describes a flow beginning inside Zeal: the user supplies an email address, confirms a code, moves assets and completes verification for the new service. Identity verification is required for access to all features, including its Visa card. Creating an account and meeting card eligibility requirements are consequently separate steps in the transition.

The companies say unsupported assets may be converted during migration, with network fees and a possible loss of some value. That means a guided move does not necessarily leave every holding unchanged. The amount received needs to be assessed after the proposed conversion and fees, rather than inferred from the balance displayed before the process begins.

A conversion also changes what a customer owns. Moving an existing asset to another interface differs from exchanging it for a different token first. The issuer and redemption structure behind stablecoins remains relevant after a wallet changes: a dollar label or familiar app does not make every token, contract and network representation equivalent.

Zeal's published supported-assets list includes ETH, the native asset of Ethereum, alongside several stablecoins and other tokens on Optimism. That is a statement about the migration route it supports, not evidence that every asset bearing the same ticker can be sent from any network. The asset and the network both need to match the receiving service's instructions.

Users can choose a different destination

Zeal also outlines alternatives for customers who do not want an ether.fi account, including sending assets to another wallet or selling and transferring money to a bank. Its instructions distinguish Smart Wallets, which it says stop working when Zeal closes, from secret-phrase or hardware wallets that can continue through other wallet apps. The applicable wallet type therefore matters to the next step.

From a business perspective, the transaction tests whether a crypto service can retain another app's users through an offered transition. Asset ownership does not guarantee that customers will join. They still have to choose a destination, complete the process and decide whether the receiving product suits their requirements. The announcement does not provide a completed migration count or evidence of subsequent customer activity.

Payment access is another separate relationship. The ether.fi page describes a Visa card, but explicitly says eligibility, availability and restrictions apply. A functioning wallet does not itself establish card approval in every jurisdiction. Similarly, a card-network brand identifies the payment rail; it does not guarantee the value of the crypto assets used alongside it.

The companies' notices do not disclose a purchase price or detailed asset valuation. The development is therefore best assessed through the confirmed operational change and the choices offered to users, rather than an invented deal size. As with other asset acquisitions, the scope of what was bought matters. Here, selected assets change hands, while customers retain the decision about where their money goes.
