Visa Adds Monad to Stablecoin Settlement as Payment Network Expands

Participating banks can use another blockchain for back-end payments, but Visa has not disclosed a Monad settlement customer or volume.

TTN Research DeskNov 10, 2026

Visa is adding another blockchain to the plumbing behind card payments. The Monad Foundation said on November 10 that participating Visa card issuers and acquirers can use its network to settle obligations in stablecoins. The change expands Visa's institutional settlement options, not the ways consumers pay at checkout. No bank using Monad for Visa settlement, or volume processed on the new rail, was disclosed.

Monad also described a planned privacy system designed to shield commercially sensitive settlement records while maintaining authorized oversight. The privacy functionality is not yet live.

What Visa's settlement integration means

Consumer purchases and institutional settlement are different parts of a payment transaction. A cardholder may make an ordinary purchase at a shop or online, with familiar payment authorization and clearing processes operating in the background. Settlement is the later movement of obligations among financial institutions and the card network.

Visa's addition of Monad concerns that back-end process. It does not require shoppers to download a cryptocurrency wallet or obtain Monad's native token. It also does not mean a store will suddenly receive its Visa proceeds as cryptocurrency.

For eligible issuers and acquirers, a new blockchain option may offer another way to transfer supported stablecoins as part of their settlement arrangements. Actual use depends on counterparties, available assets, compliance processes, custody providers and operational integration. Network support alone does not establish commercial adoption.

That is the central limit of the announcement: a supported network is not the same as a proven payment corridor. The first named institutions, production stablecoins and audited settlement totals would tell readers much more about adoption than a network listing.

Another network rather than an exclusive choice

Monad joins a group of existing settlement network options rather than displacing them. Reports on the expansion indicate that Visa's stablecoin program already included Ethereum, Solana, Avalanche, Stellar and several additional chains. The new integration broadens the menu offered to participating institutions.

That flexibility may matter because different financial institutions have different requirements around processing costs, reliability, supported assets, operational controls and ecosystem partners. An institution running a settlement process must also make sure transactions can be reconciled with its internal records and risk-management systems.

The development is part of a broader movement by payment companies toward digital settlement infrastructure. Our earlier coverage of PNC and Coinbase's institutional USDC pilot examined another way financial firms have been testing stablecoin-related processes.

Stablecoins are still the critical financial component

A blockchain can record the transfer of a token, but it cannot automatically guarantee that the token will maintain its intended value or be redeemable on demand. For an institution using dollar-linked stablecoins, questions about reserves, liquidity, issuer regulation and redemption arrangements remain central.

Our guide to stablecoin redemption and depeg risks explains why a token's stated dollar peg should not be mistaken for an unconditional guarantee. The choice of blockchain network and the credit or redemption characteristics of a stablecoin are separate dimensions of risk.

The proposed privacy feature

The Monad Foundation said a privacy-related capability is planned for the coming months. Its stated aim is to keep commercially sensitive settlement information confidential while preserving access for auditors and regulators with appropriate authorization.

Such functionality responds to a real concern in institutional finance. Payment companies may be reluctant to expose transaction amounts, counterparties or operational patterns on a network where those details could otherwise be examined publicly.

But privacy does not necessarily mean anonymity. Financial institutions need to maintain records, satisfy sanctions and financial-crime obligations, and demonstrate compliance when required. A production-ready system must define who can access information, under which circumstances and through what controls.

How much activity is taking place?

Visa said in its October earnings call that the annualized run rate for its broader stablecoin settlement program exceeded $25 billion, roughly ten times the year-earlier pace. The measure projects a recent activity rate across twelve months; it is neither a completed year's total nor a figure for Monad alone.

More importantly, this is a program-wide figure, not volume attributable to Monad. It would be misleading to assign the entire settlement rate to a newly supported network that has not published its own institutional transaction totals.

What to watch next

The most useful follow-up disclosures would name the issuers or acquirers choosing Monad, identify the stablecoins supported in production and provide verifiable figures on processed settlement. Institutions will also be interested in uptime, finality, reconciliation and the maturity of privacy controls.

For Visa, network diversity may offer partners additional flexibility. For Monad, inclusion in a major payment company's settlement options is a form of infrastructure recognition. For investors, however, the immediate financial effect cannot be quantified from the information currently available.

For now, Visa has broadened the rails available to participating institutions. Monad's commercial significance will be measured by named customers and real settlement flows, not by the size of Visa's entire stablecoin program.
