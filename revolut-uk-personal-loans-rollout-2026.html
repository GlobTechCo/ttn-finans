Revolut Enters UK Personal Lending, Putting Its Banking Ambitions to a Credit Test

The digital bank is rolling out £2,000–£25,000 unsecured loans, moving beyond payments into a business where repayment quality determines returns.

TTN Research Desk2026-10-26

Revolut has begun offering unsecured personal loans in Britain, taking its effort to become a primary bank into a business that depends on borrowers paying it back. UK chief executive Francesca Carlesi outlined the rollout to Reuters on Monday. The company's own product guidance confirms loans of £2,000 to £25,000 over one to five years, with availability expanding in stages.

The commercial opportunity differs from adding another payment feature. A loan can generate interest income over several years, but the lender must fund it, assess affordability and absorb credit losses. Revolut's move therefore tests whether a large audience for an app can translate into a durable lending relationship, rather than simply more transactions through an existing account.

For readers following banks' earnings, our recent report on deposit growth and lending margins explains why funding costs matter alongside loan growth. An attractive headline borrowing rate is only one side of a bank's economics. The other side includes what it pays for funding and what it expects customers to repay.

What the offer covers

Revolut's eligibility guide requires applicants to be UK residents aged at least 18, have a permanent UK address and phone number, and have held a Revolut bank account for more than 30 days. It also lists income above £12,000 a year, employment and a satisfactory record of managing finances. Meeting the listed conditions does not guarantee approval or the advertised rate.

The guide distinguishes an initial soft eligibility search from the hard credit search associated with a full application. It says a personalized offer depends on the applicant's circumstances and credit history. The distinction is relevant to any claim that an app-based loan is universally available: the interface can be simple while the credit decision remains selective.

Reuters reported that Revolut intends eventually to transfer much of its lending risk through securitization and keep its loan-to-deposit ratio close to 6%. That is a management ambition, not evidence that a completed transaction has already removed the new UK loans from its balance sheet.

Selling loans changes the business model

Securitization groups loans into assets against which investors can provide financing. It can release capacity and distribute some risks, but the outcome depends on the structure, retained exposures and investor demand. A lender also needs reliable information about how the underlying borrowers perform. Growing originations before that history develops can make risk harder to judge.

The new product follows Revolut's exit from UK banking-license restrictions in March. That earlier milestone enabled the bank to expand its range of services; it did not establish the profitability of each subsequent product. A regulated banking platform supplies the framework for lending, while underwriting and collections determine how a particular loan portfolio behaves over time.

Revolut's March results release reported £4.5 billion of group revenue and £1.7 billion of pretax profit for 2025. Its customer lending portfolio reached £2.2 billion, up 120% year over year, across products and markets. Those figures describe the wider group before the current UK rollout. They should not be presented as revenue or profit already generated by British personal loans.

Growth needs to survive repayment cycles

For a young portfolio, early application volumes are a poor substitute for repayment evidence. Arrears, provisions, repeat borrowing and returns after funding costs will tell investors more about whether the expansion is working. Customer acquisition can be fast; observing a full credit cycle takes longer. Our guide to lending risk and financing structures explores why where a loan is held also matters.

The distinction between a monthly payment and total borrowing cost matters for customers, too. Revolut's guidance notes that debt consolidation can cost more overall when it extends the borrowing period. An app can make comparison easier, but a lower monthly payment alone does not establish that replacing an existing debt saves money.

Monday's report gives a clearer view of Revolut's move from everyday payments toward credit. Its significance will be measured in the quality of loans that remain outstanding and the income they produce after losses, rather than the number of customers eligible to see a new button in the app.
