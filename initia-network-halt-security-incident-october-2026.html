Initia halts its blockchain after an exploit, with about $2.7 million reportedly bridged out

The project paused its main chain and connected rollups while investigating unauthorized token creation. Its co-founder’s initial estimate is not a final accounting of losses.

TTN Research Desk2026-10-23

Initia halted its main blockchain and connected rollups Friday after a security incident, interrupting an ecosystem designed to move assets between application-specific networks. Co-founder Ezaan Mangalji, known as Zon, estimated that an attacker had minted tokens without authorization and bridged out approximately $2.7 million in assets before the pause. The assessment was preliminary, with a full accounting of losses still outstanding.

The project's announcement put the halt at 3:41:30 p.m. UTC on October 23, or 11:41:30 a.m. Eastern. Initia said it was investigating with security teams, validators and exchanges. The immediate financial issue extends beyond the estimated amount moved: a stopped network can prevent ordinary transactions from settling while teams establish which assets and applications were affected.

The initial diagnosis concerns token creation

Zon said the early findings pointed to a previously unknown bug in Initia's implementation of the Move virtual machine on the Cosmos software framework. That software executes smart-contract instructions. He said the issue did not apply to the native INIT token. His comments identify the team's initial diagnosis, rather than a completed technical investigation or evidence of a flaw in every network using related software.

The estimated amount reportedly bridged out and the eventual loss are also different measurements. Investigators must establish which transactions were unauthorized, who bore the exposure and whether assets can be recovered. An amount transferred through several wallets can appear in several observations. Adding those movements together without tracing their relationship could count the same money more than once.

There is a further distinction around AUSD, a stablecoin issued by Agora. The Defiant reported that Agora described the exposure as involving a third-party product using AUSD on Ethereum and said it had no smart contracts deployed on Initia. That account should not be simplified into a claim that Agora itself issued unauthorized native AUSD on Initia or that its entire reserve backing disappeared.

An interconnected design broadens the operational impact

Initia's documentation describes its layer-1 chain as the central coordination and liquidity hub for connected rollups. Rollups are separate execution environments built around the underlying network. The architecture supplies shared services including security, governance and interoperability. Those links help explain why the announced response covered both the main chain and the surrounding rollups, rather than a single isolated application.

In general, a bridge links representations of value across networks. If one side accepts a token balance or message that should not exist, the consequences can extend to assets available on another side. Establishing the precise path matters because a successful transfer does not, by itself, identify whether the original fault sat in token creation, message validation or the receiving contract's controls.

That is why the dependencies behind decentralized-finance services deserve attention alongside transaction speed and fees. A user may interact with one application while relying on shared software, a base chain and a bridge. The practical risk includes the availability of those services, as well as the market value shown for the asset in a wallet.

A token-price rebound does not establish recovery

In its 1:31 p.m. Eastern update, The Block reported that INIT had reversed an initial 14% decline and was trading about 14% higher on the day. That was a time-specific market observation, not evidence that the network had restarted or that affected funds were recovered. Exchange trading can continue while transfers involving the underlying blockchain are restricted.

The distinction between a quoted price and usable liquidity becomes especially relevant during interruptions. A holder may see a changing exchange price while an on-chain position cannot be moved. Our explanation of crypto leverage and liquidation mechanics discusses why market liquidity, collateral and the ability to act are separate parts of a position's risk.

Stablecoin exposure also needs to be located precisely. Holding a token through a third-party bridge is a different claim from holding the issuer's directly supported version on another network. The backing and redemption structure of a stablecoin helps explain why identifying the asset, contract and route matters before drawing conclusions about the wider currency.

The announcements reviewed did not provide a verified restart timetable or a final recovery plan. The next substantive evidence will be a reconciled transaction account, a confirmed explanation of the defect and a tested path to restoring affected services. Until those details are available, the roughly $2.7 million figure describes the co-founder's early estimate, while the network halt is the confirmed operational development.
