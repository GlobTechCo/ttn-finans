Ford Raises Profit Forecast Floor as Higher Costs Test Its Recovery

Ford lifted the bottom of its annual profit forecast by $500 million. Higher commodity costs and weaker vehicle volumes complicate the improvement.

TTN Research Desk2026-10-29

Ford raised the lower end of its 2026 adjusted operating-profit forecast on Wednesday, offering a more confident earnings outlook despite declining revenue and rising raw-material costs. The automaker now expects adjusted earnings before interest and taxes of $10.5 billion to $11 billion, compared with its previous $10 billion to $11 billion range. The change improves the floor by $500 million while leaving the ceiling intact.

The distinction matters for shareholders assessing the recovery. A narrower range suggests management sees less downside to this year's underlying result. It does not amount to an equivalent increase in every possible outcome, and Ford left its annual adjusted free-cash-flow forecast at $6 billion to $7 billion. Our guide to earnings expectations explains why a better headline can coexist with a more restrained assessment of the outlook.

Profit improves on a smaller sales base

Third-quarter revenue fell 2% from a year earlier to $49.5 billion, while adjusted EBIT increased 3% to about $2.7 billion. That combination points to an improvement in the profit generated from each dollar of revenue. Chief Financial Officer Sherry House attributed the performance to a richer mix of higher-margin vehicles and cost control, rather than a broad expansion in sales.

Those gains have limits. Net income under standard accounting rules fell to $2 billion from $2.4 billion a year earlier. Adjusted EBIT excludes specified items and measures performance before interest and taxes, so it should not be substituted for net earnings. The two measures answer different questions about the quarter; reporting one without the other would exaggerate how uniformly Ford's finances improved.

The cost challenge also grew. Reuters reported that Ford increased its estimate of full-year commodity headwinds to nearly $2.5 billion from about $2 billion. Its outlook assumes U.S. industry pricing roughly 1% higher than a year earlier. That is an industry assumption within a corporate forecast, not a promise that every Ford transaction price will rise by the same amount.

The commercial and electric businesses tell different stories

Ford Pro, which serves commercial customers, earned about $1.7 billion before interest and taxes, down 16%. Ford cited higher commodities and temporary launch costs as Super Duty production began at its Oakville Assembly Complex. The gasoline and hybrid business, Ford Blue, earned about $1.4 billion, down 10%. These declines show why the companywide improvement should not be read as equal progress across the vehicle portfolio.

The electric-vehicle division, Ford Model e, narrowed its quarterly EBIT loss by 25% to about $1.1 billion. A smaller loss helps the group result, but the division remains a substantial use of earnings generated elsewhere. Ford's updated annual outlook still anticipates roughly $4 billion in Model e losses. Investors therefore have to distinguish progress toward lower losses from an established, profitable electric-vehicle operation.

Cash and investment remain separate tests

Ford generated $5.4 billion in operating cash flow and reported $4.7 billion in company-adjusted free cash flow. These figures use different boundaries and adjustments, particularly around the financing business. They cannot be compared as though the second number were simply the first minus all consolidated investment. The reconciliation accompanying the release is essential context, as our explanation of free-cash-flow definitions discusses.

Beyond vehicles, House said software services and parts produced record quarterly revenue. Ford also continues to target 20 gigawatt-hours of annual Ford Energy battery capacity online by the end of 2027. That is a future manufacturing objective, not capacity already producing the forecast financial return. The nearer test is whether recurring services and manufacturing efficiencies can offset costs without relying solely on selling a more expensive mix of trucks and SUVs.

Ford declared a regular quarterly dividend of 15 cents a share, payable December 1 to holders of record November 10. Its guidance also excludes a significant escalation in the Middle East conflict or a material U.S. downturn. Those assumptions leave a clear boundary around Wednesday's improvement: a stronger expected earnings floor within the operating conditions management currently anticipates.
