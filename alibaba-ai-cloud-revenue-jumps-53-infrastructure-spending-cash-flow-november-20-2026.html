Alibaba’s AI Cloud Revenue Jumps 53% as Infrastructure Spending Pressures Cash Flow

Alibaba's AI Cloud revenue rose 53% as capital spending climbed 60%, while free cash flow outflow widened.

TTN Research DeskNov 20, 2026 · 4:37 AM ET

Tech & AI · November 20, 2026 · 4:37 AM ET

Alibaba Group reported a sharp acceleration in artificial intelligence and cloud computing revenue for its September quarter, providing evidence that demand for commercial AI infrastructure is becoming an increasingly important source of growth for the Chinese technology company. Related context: Google Expands Its AI Chip Lineup With TPU 8tx and 8i Max as Cloud Competition Intensifies

Revenue from AI Cloud and Compute Services increased 53% year over year to approximately RMB60.97 billion, according to results released on November 19. Revenue from external customers in the segment grew 64%, while adjusted earnings before interest, taxes and amortisation increased 121%. The results show that Alibaba is beginning to convert growing demand for AI computing into higher operating income. However, the expansion remains capital-intensive. Capital expenditure increased 60% to RMB50.48 billion, while free cash flow remained negative as the company continued investing in computing infrastructure.

The figures highlight a central challenge for large technology companies competing in artificial intelligence: building profitable cloud businesses while financing the enormous infrastructure requirements of increasingly sophisticated models.

AI cloud becomes a larger contributor to Alibaba's business

Alibaba's September-quarter revenue reached RMB271.52 billion, representing a 10% increase compared with the same period a year earlier.

Alibaba said AI-related product revenue recorded triple-digit year-over-year growth for the thirteenth consecutive quarter. The company attributed the performance to increasing adoption of AI services across industries.

The development reflects a shift in the commercial AI market. Demand is no longer limited to companies building large foundational models. Enterprises are also purchasing infrastructure and services that allow them to deploy AI systems in practical applications.

Operating profitability improves alongside revenue

Alibaba reported that adjusted EBITA for its cloud segment increased 121% year over year to RMB8.84 billion. The adjusted EBITA margin reached 14.5%.

These figures suggest that revenue growth is translating into improved operating economics. A cloud provider can benefit from economies of scale when existing data centres, networking equipment and software infrastructure support a larger volume of customer workloads.

The wider group's adjusted net income increased 92% to RMB19.9 billion. However, the adjusted earnings figure should not be confused with the company's reported accounting profit, which also benefited from investment-related gains.

Infrastructure investment continues to accelerate

Capital expenditure reached RMB50.48 billion during the quarter, increasing 60% from the previous year.

The company's free cash flow was an outflow of RMB34.47 billion, compared with an outflow of RMB21.84 billion in the corresponding quarter of 2025.

Free cash flow is the cash remaining after operating expenses and capital investment. A negative figure does not necessarily indicate that a company is financially distressed, particularly when it is undertaking substantial infrastructure expansion. It does, however, show that operating cash generation is not currently covering the combined demands of the investment programme.

Alibaba reported RMB487.93 billion in cash and other liquid investments at September 30. This provides a substantial financial buffer, although the pace and efficiency of future spending will remain important considerations.

E-commerce remains a contrasting part of the business

Customer management revenue declined 5% year over year. Alibaba said it would have increased 1% on a like-for-like basis without the contra-revenue impact of its new business development program.

Revenue from China Quick Commerce rose 22% to RMB58.76 billion, while Alibaba said losses narrowed as the business maintained market share.

International e-commerce revenue fell 8%, primarily because of lower AliExpress revenue following EU customs reforms, partly offset by growth at Trendyol.

Commerce trends were mixed: quick commerce grew rapidly, while customer-management and international e-commerce revenue declined. AI cloud offers another growth engine.

Nevertheless, the company cannot rely on cloud growth alone to resolve all its operating challenges. The performance of its consumer businesses, investment requirements and broader economic conditions will continue to influence consolidated results.

US chip restrictions introduce another uncertainty

Alibaba's infrastructure strategy also faces external constraints.

Chief Executive Eddie Wu said the company's assessment of new US restrictions on advanced computing access was continuing. He indicated that the measures could affect products and services planned for the following year.

Access to advanced processors is important for training and operating large AI models. Restrictions affecting overseas data centres and certain computing arrangements could therefore influence the capacity available to Chinese technology companies.

Alibaba has also been developing its own semiconductor capabilities. Its T-Head division unveiled the Zhenwu V900 processor, with commercial availability scheduled for the first quarter of 2027.

The development illustrates how Chinese technology companies are attempting to strengthen domestic computing supply chains. However, a new processor announcement does not by itself establish that it can replace the full range of advanced imported hardware.

Performance, production capacity, software compatibility and availability will determine how much of the company's computing demand can eventually be supported by domestic chips.

What the results mean for Alibaba's AI strategy

Alibaba's latest results provide evidence that AI-related cloud services are becoming a meaningful commercial business rather than simply a long-term technology investment.

Revenue growth and higher adjusted operating earnings strengthen the case that enterprise customers are paying for computing services at an increasing scale.

At the same time, the cash-flow figures show that the economics of expansion remain demanding. The company must continue financing infrastructure while managing competition, processor availability and slower growth in parts of its traditional business.

The next important measure will be the relationship between incremental investment and incremental operating returns. Sustained revenue growth will matter, but so will the company's ability to convert that growth into durable free cash flow.

Alibaba's September quarter demonstrates progress in AI monetisation. Whether that progress can support the cost of its infrastructure ambitions will become clearer as additional capacity enters service and customer demand develops.
