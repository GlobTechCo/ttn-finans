Fidelity Files for Spot Chainlink ETF as Crypto Investment Products Expand

The proposed Nasdaq product would widen regulated crypto access while exposing investors to LINK’s volatility and custody risks.

TTN Research DeskNov 27, 2026 · 5:52 AM EST

Fidelity has filed to launch a spot exchange-traded product tracking Chainlink’s LINK token, extending the asset manager’s digital-asset range beyond bitcoin, ether and Solana. The proposal would give brokerage clients another regulated-market route to crypto exposure, but it does not mean the fund is approved or ready to trade.

The proposed Fidelity Chainlink Fund was registered with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission on November 25. The preliminary prospectus says the exchange-traded product is expected to list on Nasdaq, although the filing does not provide a confirmed launch date and the registration statement must become effective before shares can be sold. The fund would hold LINK directly and seek to track its dollar value, less expenses and liabilities. It is sponsored by FD Funds Management, a subsidiary of Fidelity parent FMR LLC.

The structure is designed to make LINK accessible through a conventional brokerage account rather than requiring investors to open a crypto exchange account, manage private keys or transfer tokens themselves. The proposed fund would use a reference rate based on prices from eligible LINK spot markets, with a volume-weighted median methodology. The filing says the index is calculated every 15 seconds using rolling market data. The trust intends to hold LINK on the Ethereum blockchain and would use Anchorage Digital Bank, BitGo Bank & Trust and Coinbase Custody as digital-asset custodians.

One important feature is what the fund will not do: it will not stake its LINK holdings. Staking can generate rewards for token holders who help secure or operate a proof-of-stake network, but the prospectus says shareholders will not receive staking rewards through the trust. The fund is designed to provide price exposure rather than a yield-bearing strategy. That distinction matters when comparing the proposed product with direct token ownership or other investment vehicles that may offer different economic rights. For Fidelity, the filing is part of a broader expansion of crypto services. The firm already offers U.S. spot products linked to bitcoin, ether and Solana, and recently added LINK, Aave and Uniswap to the list of assets available through its Fidelity Crypto trading platform, according to market reporting. The proposed Chainlink fund would add a separate exchange-traded wrapper to that distribution network. It also puts a major traditional asset manager behind an investment product focused on a token associated with blockchain infrastructure rather than a general-purpose payment or smart-contract network.

Chainlink’s role helps explain the institutional interest. The Chainlink Network provides oracle services that allow smart contracts to use external information, including market prices and other data that cannot be generated reliably on-chain. It also works on interoperability services connecting different blockchain environments. LINK is the network’s native digital asset and is used within its ecosystem. The token should not be confused with the Chainlink company or with the blockchain networks and applications that use its services.

The investment case is linked to the wider development of tokenisation and on-chain finance. If banks, asset managers and market operators increasingly represent securities, funds or other assets on blockchains, smart contracts will need dependable data about prices, collateral and events outside those networks. Oracle infrastructure can become part of that plumbing. A listed LINK product could make it easier for traditional investors to allocate to that theme without directly holding tokens. But an ETF filing does not prove that tokenisation adoption will accelerate or that LINK’s price will rise as a result.

The proposal also carries meaningful risks. The SEC prospectus warns that LINK is volatile and that investors could lose a substantial part or all of their investment. It notes that spot crypto markets are relatively new and may be more exposed to manipulation, fraud or security breaches than established securities markets. Custody is another critical issue: because LINK is a bearer asset, access depends on private keys, and loss or theft of those keys could impair the trust’s ability to access its holdings. The filing also says that the sponsor’s index methodology and eligible pricing sources may change, which could affect the value used to calculate the fund’s net asset value.

The fund’s regulatory status should be described precisely. Filing a Form S-1 is a step in the registration process, not an SEC endorsement of LINK and not final permission to begin trading. The preliminary prospectus remains subject to change. It does not yet specify a management fee or a definitive trading commencement date. Investors should not treat the proposed Nasdaq ticker or expected listing as confirmation that the product has been approved.

Competition is already developing. Grayscale and Bitwise have introduced Chainlink investment products, according to The Block, so Fidelity would enter a market that is not entirely new. Its potential advantage is distribution: the Fidelity name, existing brokerage relationships and established custody partners could make the product more accessible to advisers and investors who prefer exchange-traded securities to direct crypto ownership. Whether that translates into meaningful assets under management will depend on the fee, liquidity, market conditions and investor demand once the product is approved.

The filing is another sign that traditional finance is building a wider menu of crypto-linked products. The important question is no longer only whether large asset managers will offer bitcoin exposure, but which networks and infrastructure tokens they consider suitable for regulated investment wrappers. Fidelity’s Chainlink proposal is a concrete step in that direction, while remaining a proposal rather than a launched fund.
