Fidelity Files for Spot Chainlink ETF as Crypto Investment Products Expand

The proposed Nasdaq product would widen regulated crypto access while exposing investors to LINK’s volatility and custody risks.

TTN Research DeskNov 27, 2026 · 5:52 AM EST

Fidelity has filed a registration statement for a proposed spot investment product holding Chainlink’s LINK token, adding another major asset manager to a market that already has listed LINK products. The filing gives the proposal a clearer structure, but it does not mean the fund has been approved, has begun trading or has a confirmed launch date.

The Fidelity Chainlink Fund filed a Form S-1 with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission on November 25. The prospectus says the shares are expected to trade on Nasdaq, subject to the registration statement becoming effective and the exchange’s listing process. It uses the term exchange-traded product. That distinction matters: the trust explicitly says it is not registered as an investment company under the Investment Company Act of 1940 and therefore does not carry the same regulatory framework and protections as a conventional 1940 Act mutual fund or ETF.

The filing identifies [FLNK] as the proposed trading symbol, but the brackets in the preliminary prospectus indicate that the designation is not final. It also leaves the annual sponsor-fee percentage blank. The document describes a unified fee that would accrue daily against the trust’s LINK holdings and be paid monthly in LINK or cash. Until Fidelity files an amended prospectus with completed terms, investors cannot compare the fund’s eventual cost with competing products on a like-for-like basis.

The trust is designed to hold LINK directly and track its dollar value through the Fidelity Chainlink Reference Rate, less expenses and liabilities. The index uses prices from eligible LINK spot markets and a volume-weighted median methodology, calculated every 15 seconds from rolling market data. The trust intends to hold LINK on the Ethereum blockchain, even though the token exists across multiple blockchain environments. Anchorage Digital Bank, BitGo Bank & Trust and Coinbase Custody are named as digital-asset custodians.

One notable design choice is that the trust will not stake its LINK. Chainlink staking allows eligible participants to commit tokens in support of certain oracle services and may generate rewards, but the prospectus says the fund will not stake its holdings and shareholders will not receive staking income. The proposed product is therefore a price-exposure vehicle, not a yield strategy. Its LINK balance per share will also decline gradually as tokens are used to pay the sponsor fee and other liabilities, even if the market price of LINK is unchanged.

Fidelity is not entering an empty category. Grayscale’s Chainlink Trust, trading as GLNK, and Bitwise’s Chainlink ETF, trading as CLNK, already provide listed exposure to the token. The Block reported that the products held about $112 million and $42 million in assets, respectively, on November 25. Fidelity had also added LINK to its retail crypto trading platform the day before the filing, giving the proposed fund a route into an existing customer base. Its potential advantage is distribution, but the outcome will depend on the final fee, liquidity, market-maker participation and whether advisers and clients choose another issuer’s product. Recent flows into U.S. spot Bitcoin funds, covered in TTN Finance’s ETF outflow report, show how quickly demand for crypto-linked products can change.

The underlying asset is different from the fund wrapper. Chainlink is an oracle and interoperability network that helps smart contracts obtain external information and communicate across blockchain environments. LINK is the network’s native digital asset, used to pay for certain services and compensate node operators and other service providers. It is not a share in Chainlink Labs, nor is it a claim on the revenues of every application that uses the network. The distinction is important when assessing the investment thesis: growth in tokenisation or decentralised finance does not automatically translate into demand for LINK.

The prospectus highlights substantial risks. LINK has experienced sharp price volatility, and investors could lose most or all of their investment. The trust depends on custodians safeguarding private keys; a loss, theft or operational failure could impair access to its holdings. Spot crypto markets may be vulnerable to manipulation, fraud and security incidents. The trust’s shares could also trade at a premium or discount to net asset value, and market liquidity may weaken if authorised participants or market makers withdraw.

The regulatory structure also deserves attention. The S-1 registers the proposed securities offering under the Securities Act, but the trust says it is not registered under the Investment Company Act of 1940. It is also not a commodity pool, and the sponsor says it is not acting as an investment adviser subject to the Advisers Act fiduciary standard. Those disclosures do not make the product unlawful; they define which investor protections do and do not apply. Investors comparing crypto wrappers should read those differences alongside the broader history of spot Bitcoin products and institutional access.

The filing is another sign that traditional asset managers are extending crypto exposure beyond bitcoin and ether. But Fidelity’s proposal is still a preliminary document, with a blank fee, a provisional ticker and no confirmed trading date. The practical test will be whether the final product offers competitive economics and reliable liquidity. Until the SEC registration becomes effective and Nasdaq confirms the listing, the fund remains a proposed vehicle rather than a new source of live market demand for LINK.
