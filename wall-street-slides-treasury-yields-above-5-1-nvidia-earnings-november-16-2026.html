Wall Street Slides as Treasury Yields Stay Above 5.1% Ahead of Nvidia Results

U.S. equities weakened as Treasury yields stayed above 5.1%, leaving investors focused on Nvidia's results and the outlook for AI spending.

TTN Research DeskNov 16, 2026

U.S. stocks came under renewed pressure on Monday as investors weighed elevated Treasury yields, a broad retreat in technology shares and uncertainty ahead of Nvidia's quarterly results. The decline was concentrated in AI-related and semiconductor stocks, leaving the Nasdaq more exposed than the broader market.

The 10-year U.S. Treasury yield was around 5.14% in Charles Schwab's 9:14 a.m. ET market update. Investors were also preparing for Nvidia's fiscal third-quarter report on Tuesday, November 17. The company has become a key gauge of spending on artificial-intelligence infrastructure, so its outlook could influence sentiment well beyond the semiconductor industry.

Treasury yields remain above 5.1%

A 5.14% yield keeps the bond market central to the equity outlook. Higher long-term yields can increase borrowing costs, make government debt more competitive with shares and reduce the present value investors assign to future corporate earnings. That effect can be especially visible in growth stocks, whose valuations depend heavily on profits expected years ahead.

The yield level should not be confused with a single official daily Treasury close. Schwab's figure was an intraday market snapshot, while the U.S. Treasury's latest published daily par-yield observation available at the time was 5.15% for Friday, November 13. The two readings describe different points in time.

The elevated level of yields has also sharpened debate about the Federal Reserve's next move. Recent inflation data and expectations for a potentially restrictive policy path have made investors more cautious about assets that are sensitive to financing costs. TTN Finance previously examined the inflation backdrop in its report on the October U.S. CPI increase and Treasury yields.

Technology and semiconductor shares lead losses

Reuters reported that by 9:58 a.m. ET the Dow Jones Industrial Average was down 0.32%, the S&P 500 had fallen 0.82% and the Nasdaq Composite had lost 1.32%. Nvidia shares were lower by about 1.6% in that market snapshot, while semiconductor stocks also weakened.

The pressure reflects a market reassessing the cost and expected return of the AI investment cycle. Cloud providers and technology groups are committing substantial sums to data centers, processors, networking equipment and power infrastructure. Investors increasingly want evidence that those expenses will produce lasting revenue and cash flow.\n\nThat sensitivity extends beyond public equities. Companies funding data-center construction with debt face higher interest expense when yields reset or refinancing comes due. Utilities, infrastructure operators and technology firms can therefore experience different effects from the same move in long-term rates, depending on leverage, contract structures and the timing of capital spending.

That does not mean demand for AI computing has suddenly disappeared. It means the bar for company results and forward guidance is high, particularly when borrowing costs remain elevated. TTN Finance has covered an earlier period of AI-related selling and changing rate expectations in its report on the Wall Street AI selloff and Fed-rate debate.

Nvidia's report becomes the week's main test

Nvidia is scheduled to release its third-quarter fiscal 2027 results on Tuesday, November 17, at approximately 1:20 p.m. Pacific Time. The company has scheduled its earnings call for 2 p.m. Pacific, or 5 p.m. Eastern. The reporting period ended October 25.

Investors will look beyond headline revenue and earnings. Data-center demand, product availability, supply constraints, margins and the pace of customer investment are likely to shape the market's response. Management's comments about the next quarter may matter as much as the reported results: a strong performance can still disappoint if the outlook falls short of elevated expectations.

U.S. policy added another variable on Monday. Reuters reported that Washington had unveiled new interim rules tightening China's access to advanced AI chips from companies such as Nvidia. The measures could affect the company's addressable market and supply-chain planning, alongside any future changes in licensing or enforcement.

What investors will watch next

The immediate questions are whether Treasury yields remain near current levels, whether weakness spreads beyond technology and whether Nvidia's outlook supports expectations for continued AI infrastructure spending. A stabilization in bond yields could ease one source of pressure, but it would not remove the need for strong earnings and credible guidance.

Monday's trading illustrates the tension facing investors: demand for AI infrastructure remains a major growth theme, yet higher yields and demanding valuations leave little room for disappointment. Nvidia's results may provide a clearer view of corporate spending, but they will not settle the wider debate over how much investors should pay for future growth.
