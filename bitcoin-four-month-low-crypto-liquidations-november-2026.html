Bitcoin Falls to a Four-Month Low as Crypto Liquidations Reach $2 Billion

Bitcoin dropped to its weakest level since July as forced selling spread across digital assets. The scale of liquidations highlights how leverage has amplified the market decline.

TTN Research DeskNov 15, 2026

Bitcoin extends its decline

Bitcoin fell to a four-month low on Sunday, November 15, as a broad cryptocurrency sell-off continued across Asian trading hours. The largest digital asset by market value reached $62,112, its weakest level since July 14, according to Bloomberg reporting published by Business Standard.

The decline followed a sharp fall during the previous US trading session. Bitcoin lost 5.7% on Saturday in the United States before dropping a further 3.6% in Asian trading on Sunday, according to the report. Ether also weakened, reaching $1,711, its lowest level since July 8.

The move extended a decline that had already erased about 25% of Bitcoin's value since October 21. The speed of the sell-off was important because it forced leveraged traders to close positions as prices moved against them. Those forced sales added supply to an already falling market.

Liquidations magnify the move

The most striking feature of the weekend decline was the volume of forced liquidations. Exchanges tracked by CoinGlass recorded approximately $2 billion in cryptocurrency positions liquidated during the 24 hours through 9 a.m. Sunday in Singapore, according to Bloomberg.

Bitcoin positions accounted for about $774 million of that total, while Ether positions represented approximately $488 million. Long positions made up the vast majority of the liquidations. These are trades that benefit when prices rise, so their forced closure during a sharp decline can intensify downward pressure.

The figures show why leverage can make cryptocurrency markets move more abruptly than the underlying change in investor demand might suggest. A trader who borrows to increase exposure must maintain sufficient collateral. When the market moves against that position, an exchange can close it automatically to limit further losses. The weekend's reported liquidation total followed another large event on Friday. Bloomberg cited approximately $1.3 billion in long cryptocurrency bets liquidated over the preceding 24 hours. The two episodes indicate that forced selling has been a recurring feature of the downturn rather than an isolated market disruption.

Liquidation data measure positions closed by exchanges, not the total amount of money permanently lost by all market participants. The numbers also depend on the exchanges and time windows included in the calculation.

ETF outflows add to institutional pressure

The decline has taken place alongside substantial withdrawals from US-listed spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds. Bloomberg reported that these products recorded almost $3 billion in net outflows between October 30 and November 13.

That period ended before the weekend price decline. US ETF flow data should therefore not be presented as a fresh November 15 trading-session figure. The distinction matters because traditional US markets were closed over the weekend, while cryptocurrency trading continued.

ETF outflows can indicate that investors are reducing exposure through regulated investment products. They do not, by themselves, explain every move in the underlying Bitcoin market. Investors may sell ETF shares for portfolio, liquidity or risk-management reasons, while activity on cryptocurrency exchanges and derivatives platforms continues independently.

Macro conditions remain a headwind

Market participants have also pointed to expectations of higher interest rates, a stronger risk-off mood and uncertainty surrounding technology-sector spending. These factors can affect digital assets because investors often treat Bitcoin and other cryptocurrencies as risk-sensitive holdings, particularly during periods when the expected return on safer assets becomes more attractive.

The relationship is not mechanical. Bitcoin can move independently of equities and interest rates over shorter periods, and a simultaneous decline does not establish that one market caused the other to fall. But tighter financial conditions can make investors less willing to hold volatile assets or maintain leveraged exposure.

The market has also been dealing with the continuing fallout from a reported $6 billion Binance security incident. Bloomberg included that event among the pressures weighing on sentiment. It should be treated as part of the broader background rather than proof that the incident directly caused Sunday's price low.

Another possible influence is the expected public offering of AI company Anthropic. Richard Galvin of DACM told Bloomberg that capital may be shifting toward the anticipated offering, potentially making cryptocurrency a short-term source of funds for some investors. That is an analyst interpretation, not a verified measurement of money moving directly from Bitcoin into the IPO.

What the decline says about market structure

That does not establish that Bitcoin's longer-term investment case has changed. Nor does a four-month low prove that the market has reached a bottom. Price levels can stabilize after a liquidation event, but a sustained recovery would require evidence that buyers are willing to absorb supply and that forced selling is easing.

For investors, the next indicators to watch include spot-market trading activity, US ETF flows when the next reporting session resumes, derivatives open interest and the balance between long and short liquidations. A decline in liquidations could indicate that the immediate leverage-driven phase is cooling, although it would not guarantee a price recovery.

The market will also need to show whether demand returns after the weekend. If Bitcoin remains under pressure when traditional US markets reopen, investors will have a clearer indication of whether the latest decline was primarily a derivatives-driven event or part of a broader reduction in risk exposure.
