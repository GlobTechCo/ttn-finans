China Uses American AI Models to Train Domestic Rivals as Chip Restrictions Tighten

Chinese researchers and technology firms are increasingly using model distillation on outputs from leading U.S. AI systems to train domestic alternatives despite tightening chip export curbs.

TTN Research DeskNov 17, 2026 · 2:19 PM EET

Chinese researchers and companies are increasingly using outputs from leading American artificial intelligence models to improve domestic alternatives, according to a Reuters investigation published on Tuesday. The practice, known as model distillation, is becoming more widespread as Beijing seeks to expand AI adoption while facing tighter restrictions on access to advanced computing hardware.

A review of more than 300 Chinese academic papers, patents, corporate disclosures, recruitment notices and government documents found that distillation has moved beyond isolated research projects into broader industrial use.

The findings highlight an important development in the global AI competition. Restrictions on advanced chips can limit the computing resources available to Chinese developers, but they do not prevent those developers from learning from the outputs of models trained elsewhere.

How model distillation works

Model distillation is a training technique in which a more capable system acts as a teacher for a smaller model. The teacher generates answers, explanations, code or demonstrations. Developers use those outputs as training examples for a student model, which learns patterns and attempts to reproduce selected capabilities.

For developers facing limited access to advanced processors, the economic appeal is straightforward. A powerful model can solve a difficult problem once, while its output can be reused as training material for a smaller system.

Distillation does not remove the need for computing infrastructure. Training, testing and deployment still require hardware, and a student system may not reproduce the teacher's full reasoning ability or general performance. It can, however, make available computing resources more productive by focusing training on selected capabilities.

Research examples show how the method is being applied

The Reuters review identified examples involving American models and Chinese research institutions or companies.

A November 2024 paper from researchers at Shanghai Jiao Tong University described selecting approximately 5,000 answers generated by OpenAI's o1 model. Researchers translated the material using GPT-4o mini and assembled 10,750 bilingual training examples to further train Alibaba's Qwen2.5-72B model.

Alibaba researchers described a more systematic process in an April 2025 paper. They used GPT-4 and GPT-4o to generate training material in languages other than Chinese, then refined and scored responses before using them to train smaller Qwen models.

Another example appeared in a February 2026 paper involving researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University and food-delivery company Meituan. The project used GPT-4o to generate plans for coordinating AI agents on programming problems, helping train a smaller model to organize similar tasks. These examples show that distillation extends beyond question-and-answer datasets to support programming, multilingual responses and AI agent coordination.

Beijing sees distillation as an industrial tool

Chinese government policies have increasingly emphasized AI use across manufacturing and other industries. A national AI-plus-manufacturing policy package published in January by eight government agencies includes guidance supporting model training, fine-tuning and distillation. Authorities in Shanghai and Shenzhen have also encouraged techniques intended to reduce computing requirements.

Smaller systems can be more practical for manufacturing, logistics and enterprise software when designed for specific tasks, as lower operating costs make it easier to deploy AI across multiple production sites or business units. However, researchers have also warned that excessive dependence on foreign model outputs could weaken original innovation and leave domestic developers reliant on breakthroughs made elsewhere.

The practice is becoming a geopolitical issue

The expansion of distillation has created friction between Washington and Beijing. U.S. authorities have accused Chinese companies of using aggressive and targeted distillation of American AI models on an industrial scale. During meetings in Chengdu in July, U.S. Commerce Department officials raised concerns with Chinese regulators about collection of outputs from leading American models.

Chinese officials defended distillation as a standard AI-development technique and indicated they were open to action where conduct constituted intellectual-property theft. Distillation itself is not illegal and is used internationally; the dispute centers on how outputs are obtained, the scale of collection and whether particular activities violate intellectual-property rights or contractual terms. OpenAI and Anthropic both restrict use of their model outputs to train competing systems without permission, creating legal uncertainty for companies relying on third-party responses. Chip restrictions and the shifting AI race

The investigation arrives as the United States moves to tighten hardware controls. On Monday, the Commerce Department introduced rules intended to restrict remote access to advanced computing chips and servers through data centers located in third countries, targeting arrangements that allow companies in restricted markets to rent computing capacity without physically importing controlled hardware. While export controls target infrastructure needed to train and operate frontier models, distillation allows developers to reuse capabilities produced by systems that already have access to that hardware. The growing use of distillation suggests that AI competitiveness increasingly depends not only on advanced chips, but also on data quality, training methods and deployment efficiency. As Washington and Beijing compete over chips, data and intellectual property, control over model outputs has become another front in the global technology contest.
