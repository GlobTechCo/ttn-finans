Crypto Industry Enters US Midterms After $176 Million Political Spending Push

Reuters identified about $176 million in election-cycle spending. Control of Congress could shape the rules exchanges and token issuers face next.

TTN Research Desk2026-11-02

Crypto industry groups have spent about $176 million on US congressional races this election cycle, Reuters reported ahead of the November 3 midterms. The spending spans more than 100 contests and reflects an effort to shape the next Congress's approach to digital-asset regulation. The outcome will depend not only on which candidates win, but also on which proposals lawmakers can advance.

What the spending figure means

The total covers election-cycle spending identified in Reuters’ investigation, rather than spending on November 2 alone. It includes Fairshake and affiliated political organizations.

According to Reuters, Fairshake and affiliates had spent about $150.8 million through October 20, with additional crypto organisations contributing roughly $25 million. The industry has supported candidates across party lines, favouring politicians considered receptive to its regulatory goals. This strategy reflects a longer-term effort to develop influence in Congress rather than a single election-day bet.

Super PACs can accept large donations and make independent political expenditures under US rules, but they cannot coordinate their spending directly with a candidate's campaign. Their advertisements often focus on general political issues rather than cryptocurrency. This matters because a viewer may not understand that financial and technology interests are financing a message that appears unrelated to digital-asset regulation.

A strategic change for the industry

Cryptocurrency companies once often positioned themselves as outsiders to the traditional financial system. Their political activity now resembles the strategies of established industries that seek predictable legislation and a say in regulatory design. Reuters identified businesses and investors associated with Coinbase, Ripple and Andreessen Horowitz among significant sources of financial support for the wider effort.

Why Congress matters to investors

Congress can set the statutory boundaries for digital-asset supervision, including market-structure legislation, exchange registration and agency authority. Committee control and legislative priorities influence which proposals can advance.

Investors may respond to election outcomes by reassessing the probability of new legislation. However, an election result is not a regulation, and a bill passing one chamber is not equivalent to becoming law. The details of committee control, legislative priorities and party divisions may matter more than a simple headline about which party wins.

What the election cannot immediately change

Reuters reported that 53 of the 58 candidates supported by Fairshake in primaries or special elections advanced to the November ballot. That figure shows the PAC's ability to select and support viable candidates; it does not reveal what proportion of those victories was caused by its spending. Separating correlation from causation is essential in evaluating corporate political influence.

The transparency question

Advocacy groups, including Public Citizen, have criticised the scale of corporate election expenditure. The underlying concern extends beyond cryptocurrency: voters need to know who is financing advertisements and what policy objectives donors may pursue afterward. Financial disclosures and independent monitoring provide a basis for scrutiny, but the organisations' strategies can still be difficult for ordinary voters to track.

What to watch after November 3

The first useful indicators will be the composition of the House and Senate, leadership of relevant committees and statements by legislators about financial-market priorities. Subsequently, investors should track the introduction of specific bills, their consumer-protection provisions, committee hearings and any realistic timetable for passage.
