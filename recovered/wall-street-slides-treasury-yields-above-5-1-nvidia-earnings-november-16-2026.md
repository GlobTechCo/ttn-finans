# Wall Street Slides as Treasury Yields Stay Above 5.1% Ahead of Nvidia Results

U.S. stocks moved lower on Monday as investors weighed elevated Treasury yields against growing uncertainty ahead of Nvidia's quarterly earnings report. Technology and semiconductor shares led the decline, adding pressure to the Nasdaq as markets entered one of the most closely watched weeks of the corporate reporting calendar.

The 10-year U.S. Treasury yield remained above 5.1%, keeping borrowing costs and equity valuations in focus. At the same time, traders were preparing for Nvidia's results, scheduled for Tuesday, November 17. The company has become a central indicator of spending across the artificial intelligence industry, making its financial performance and outlook particularly important for technology investors.

The market's reaction reflected two connected concerns: whether higher yields will continue to weigh on growth-oriented shares and whether the latest results from the largest AI infrastructure supplier can justify the expectations built into its valuation.

## Treasury yields keep pressure on equity valuations

The 10-year Treasury yield was around 5.14% in an early Monday market snapshot published by Charles Schwab. The U.S. Treasury's latest official daily par-yield data available at the time showed a 5.15% yield for November 13. These figures represent different observations and should not be treated as identical intraday measurements.

Higher long-term yields can affect equities through several channels. They raise the return investors can receive from government debt, increase financing costs across the economy and reduce the present value of future corporate earnings. The effect is particularly important for companies whose valuations depend heavily on expected growth many years into the future.

That dynamic can make technology shares more sensitive to changes in bond yields than companies with stronger near-term cash flows or higher dividend distributions. When yields rise, investors may demand a larger return for holding riskier assets, potentially putting pressure on valuations even when corporate earnings remain solid.

The latest move in Treasury yields also comes after a period of debate over the Federal Reserve's policy outlook. Investors have been assessing inflation data, employment conditions and the possibility that interest rates could remain restrictive for longer than previously expected.

## Technology shares lead the decline

Major U.S. stock indexes were lower during Monday morning trading. Reuters reported that by 9:58 a.m. Eastern Time, the Dow Jones Industrial Average had fallen 0.32%, the S&P 500 was down 0.82% and the Nasdaq Composite had declined 1.32%.

The weakness was more pronounced in semiconductor shares. Nvidia stock was down around 1.6% in the same Reuters market update, while the broader semiconductor index also came under pressure.

The concentration of losses in technology and chip stocks suggests that investors were adjusting exposure to sectors most closely associated with the AI investment cycle. The decline does not necessarily indicate that demand for AI infrastructure is weakening. Instead, it highlights how sensitive the sector has become to valuation, financing conditions and expectations for future spending.

The broader market has also been navigating concerns about the sustainability of large technology companies' capital expenditure plans. Data centers, advanced processors, networking equipment and electricity infrastructure require substantial investment. Investors are increasingly looking for evidence that these expenses will translate into durable revenue and earnings rather than simply expanding capacity.

This issue has become a recurring theme in the market's assessment of AI-related companies. ## Nvidia earnings become the week's main test

Nvidia is scheduled to publish its third-quarter fiscal 2027 financial results on November 17. The reporting period ended October 25. According to the company's investor-relations announcement, the results are expected after the U.S. market close, with a conference call scheduled for 5 p.m. Eastern Time.

The report will be closely watched because Nvidia occupies a central position in the supply chain for advanced AI computing. Its processors and related systems are used by cloud providers, large technology companies and other organizations expanding their AI infrastructure.

Investors will be looking beyond the headline revenue and earnings figures. Important questions include the pace of data-center demand, the availability of advanced components, supply constraints and the company's ability to maintain margins as competition and customer investment plans evolve.

The market will also examine management's comments about future demand. A strong quarter may not be sufficient to support the share price if the company's outlook falls short of elevated expectations. Conversely, confidence in continued spending by major cloud and AI customers could help stabilize sentiment across semiconductor stocks.

The earnings report also arrives amid continuing uncertainty over U.S. restrictions on advanced chip exports to China. Regulatory developments and changes in the addressable market remain relevant to Nvidia's longer-term growth prospects.

## What investors will watch next

The immediate market direction will depend on several factors. Treasury yields will remain important because a sustained move above current levels could add pressure to growth-oriented equities. Nvidia's results and management commentary will provide a fresh indication of demand across the AI infrastructure market.

Investors will also monitor whether weakness remains concentrated in semiconductor shares or spreads to a wider range of sectors. A broader decline could indicate that concerns about financing costs and economic conditions are becoming more significant than the upcoming earnings report alone.

For now, Monday's trading reflects a market balancing strong expectations for AI-related growth against a less supportive interest-rate environment. Nvidia's report may provide a clearer picture of corporate demand, but it will not resolve the broader question of how much investors are willing to pay for future growth while Treasury yields remain elevated.
