# Nvidia Faces First Earnings Without Jensen Huang as Trump Backs Possible Return

Nvidia is preparing to report its quarterly results under interim leadership for the first time since Jensen Huang stepped down as chief executive, adding an unusual leadership question to an earnings announcement already expected to attract significant attention from investors.

The company is scheduled to publish its third-quarter fiscal 2027 results on Tuesday, November 17. The report will be the first major financial update since Nvidia announced that Huang had resigned as president, chief executive and a member of the board. Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress was appointed interim chief executive while the board began searching for a permanent successor.

The leadership transition has added uncertainty around the world's most closely watched AI chipmaker. On Monday, U.S. President Donald Trump said he would not mind seeing Huang return to the company, following reports that the former chief executive could be considering a comeback.

Trump's comments do not represent a formal company decision or an announced government initiative. Nvidia has not publicly confirmed that Huang will return.

## A major leadership change before earnings

Nvidia announced on November 12 that Huang had resigned from his positions, effective immediately. The company's announcement named Kress as interim CEO and said the board had begun a search for a permanent chief executive.

The announcement marked a significant change for a company whose public identity and strategic direction have been closely associated with Huang. He has led Nvidia through its transformation from a graphics-chip manufacturer into a central supplier of computing infrastructure for artificial intelligence.

The timing makes Tuesday's earnings report particularly important. Investors will be assessing not only revenue and profitability but also whether the company's strategy, customer relationships and product roadmap remain stable during the leadership transition.

Nvidia's regulatory filing with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission also documented the executive change. The filing provides the formal corporate record of the resignation and interim appointment, separate from subsequent media speculation about a possible return.

The company has not announced a permanent successor. Until it provides further information, Kress remains the executive named to lead the business on an interim basis.

## Trump says he would not oppose Huang's return

Trump said on Monday that he would not mind seeing Huang return to Nvidia. The remarks followed reporting that Huang could be considering a comeback after leaving the company.

The statement is politically notable, given Nvidia's strategic importance to the U.S. technology sector and the broader debate over advanced semiconductor manufacturing, exports and competition with China. However, Trump's comments should not be interpreted as evidence that Nvidia's board has changed its position or that a return is being negotiated.

A chief executive appointment is a corporate governance matter. Nvidia's board is responsible for overseeing the leadership process and determining who will hold the permanent position.

Reports about a possible return have also drawn attention to the circumstances surrounding Huang's departure. Those reports and related allegations should be treated as unconfirmed unless supported by formal company disclosures or independently established evidence.

For investors, the practical question is whether uncertainty around the leadership transition will affect decision-making, customer confidence or the company's ability to execute its product strategy.

## Earnings expectations remain unusually high

Nvidia's third-quarter fiscal 2027 report is scheduled for November 17. The company said results would be released after the market close, with a conference call planned for 5 p.m. Eastern Time.

Reuters reported that analysts were expecting adjusted earnings per share of approximately $2.47 and revenue of about $109.08 billion. These are market expectations, not company-reported results or official guidance.

The figures reflect the scale of demand that investors have come to expect from Nvidia's data-center business. Major cloud providers and technology companies continue to invest in computing infrastructure needed to train and operate AI models.

Investors will be looking for evidence that demand remains strong and that Nvidia can convert it into revenue while maintaining profitability. Supply availability, product delivery schedules, customer spending plans and gross margins are likely to feature prominently in the discussion.

The outlook may matter more than the headline result. When expectations are high, a company can report substantial year-over-year growth and still disappoint the market if its forward guidance is weaker than investors anticipated.

## AI infrastructure remains the central business issue

Nvidia's longer-term prospects depend on more than the performance of one quarterly reporting period. The company is competing in a market where customers are building large-scale data centers and evaluating the economics of AI infrastructure.

The next phase of investment will depend on whether businesses can generate sufficient returns from AI applications to justify continued spending on processors, networking systems, power and data-center capacity.

Investors will also be watching the transition to Nvidia's next generation of products, including the Vera Rubin platform. The timing of deployments, supply-chain readiness and customer adoption will help determine how smoothly the company can move from its current product cycle to future systems.

Export restrictions remain another important factor. U.S. rules governing advanced semiconductor sales to China have complicated the addressable market for Nvidia and other chipmakers. Any changes in policy or licensing conditions could affect revenue opportunities and supply-chain planning.

## What investors will watch during the call

Tuesday's earnings call will offer an opportunity to assess how the leadership transition is being managed. Investors will be looking for clarity on Kress's role, the board's search process and whether the company's strategic priorities remain unchanged.

The financial questions will be equally important: data-center revenue, margins, demand visibility, supply constraints and the outlook for the next quarter. Management's comments on customer investment plans could influence sentiment across the wider semiconductor and AI sectors.

The leadership story may dominate headlines, but Nvidia's valuation ultimately depends on its ability to deliver financial results and maintain its competitive position. A possible return by Huang remains speculation unless the company confirms it.

For now, Nvidia faces two tests at once: demonstrating that its business momentum remains intact and reassuring investors that a sudden change at the top will not disrupt its execution.
