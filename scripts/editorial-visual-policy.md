# TTN editorial image policy

## Mandatory style rule

Never use a cinematic style for TTN imagery. Apply this to every new cover, illustration, regeneration and replacement. Inspect the actual output: a prompt saying “no cinematic” does not establish compliance. Reject and regenerate any result with film-like grading, dramatic lighting, warm/cool color separation, artificial glow, heavy contrast or polished movie-frame staging before publication. Use natural editorial photography aesthetics or a clearly designed editorial graphic when appropriate. This restriction does not require making all images gray or removing relevant people.

Apply alongside `.agents/skills/ttn-editorial/SKILL.md`, the project-wide rules. This file owns image acceptance and correction approvals.

## Mandatory quality threshold: 8/10

The owner explicitly requires a minimum self-assessed quality of 8/10 for every TTN generated image, including previews presented as candidates and published covers. As clarified by the owner, this threshold primarily measures how well the card's image matches the article's main event, meaning and editorial angle, and how clearly and engagingly it communicates that story. It is not a rating of image dimensions, resolution or display sizes. Never knowingly present a lower-rated generation as a deliverable or ask the owner to perform the first quality check.

Before generating, review the concept for its direct connection to the news, visual clarity, originality relative to recent covers and feasibility of accurate details. After generation, inspect the actual output at full article size and card size. A promising prompt or concept score cannot substitute for reviewing the result.

Record an internal editorial-fit score out of 10: does the image identify the main company, product or event; match the specific news angle and its confirmed status; communicate the story clearly at a glance; and offer a thoughtful, compelling visual rather than an interchangeable worker, office or decorative device? Require at least 8/10 for this substantive fit independently of technical execution. Then check factual/brand/architectural and anatomical credibility, composition, framing, technical quality and the established visual style as separate acceptance conditions. Excellent sharpness or correct dimensions cannot compensate for weak relevance. Incorrect branding, invented named architecture, cropped heads or essential objects, unreadable key content, anatomical errors and cinematic treatment fail the gate regardless of the score.

Reject and revise or regenerate a failing result autonomously before presenting it as ready or publishing it. Do not inflate scores to meet the threshold. The score is editorial judgment, not an objective guarantee of audience response. Keep scores and rejected attempts internal unless the owner requests an assessment; do not add ratings to public articles. Existing approval requirements for requested image corrections still apply to the specific accepted preview.

- Use neutral natural daylight, realistic white balance/exposure and believable color. Exclude cinematic/moody grading, dramatic skies, film grain, teal/orange effects, strong vignettes, artificial glow and harsh glare. Neutral does not mean gray or desaturated.
- Exclude staged advertising behavior: posed agent-and-buyer groups, handshakes, matching smiles and showroom-perfect settings. For housing-market coverage, prefer ordinary residential construction and credible everyday activity. Include people when the scene calls for them without inventing named participants.
- Inspect the actual result for anatomy, perspective, architecture, signage, equipment and plausible placement. Reject weak results and regenerate before seeking approval. Keep keypads readable with digits/symbols, equipment intact and vehicles parked realistically.
- Compare recent covers for repeated settings, office meetings, round devices, gas nozzles and recurring faces. Choose a distinct scene suited to the article.
- Preserve generated-image provenance internally and use truthful alt/schema. Do not add the unwanted visible AI-generation caption or misrepresent a reconstruction as an original news photograph.

- October 19 correction: avoid recurring faces and near-identical people across covers. Changing glasses or hair alone does not create sufficient variety. Vary age, facial structure, skin tone, build, clothing, role, pose, camera angle and setting where people are appropriate; use a different subject when it fits the story better. Compare recent covers before accepting a new image. Never describe synthetic people as actual participants in the reported event.

- After the user requests a correction to a generated image, produce and show the revised preview first. Wait for explicit approval of that specific preview before replacing any website, homepage or social-card image. Agreement to this workflow is not approval of an unseen image.
- Check company recognition at article-card size. For office imagery, prefer a documented entrance/facade angle with its authentic installed brand sign clearly readable. A distant aerial view or an unmarked facade is insufficient when the company is the story. Do not invent a sign to solve this: find another documented location or angle.
- For unfamiliar companies, the headline and short deck must explain the company's role, the concrete event and why readers should care in plain English. Do not lead with unexplained company names, acronyms, financing jargon or phrases such as "model stack" when the news is an acquisition of software-security tools.

- Select the subject for the specific article before choosing a scene. Avoid recurring office-table scenes, interchangeable people and decorative technology hardware.
- When depicting a named company, bank, office, campus or identifiable city architecture, identify the real location and obtain documentary photographic references. Record the source and location. Preserve the actual building form, facade, installed signage and surrounding layout. Do not substitute an invented building or place a company logo on a generic monitor, tablet or paper as a default illustration.
- Inspect the generated result against the reference before publication. A recognizable logo does not establish that the architecture is accurate. Reject unsupported architectural additions. Record any remaining uncertainty; do not describe a generated reconstruction as a documentary photograph or an image of the reported event.
- Include people when they belong in the scene: customers and staff in shops, pedestrians in urban settings, and people working where relevant. Use varied ages, appearances, clothing and compositions. Neither remove people categorically nor force them into every image. For reference-faithful architecture, retain the documented setting and avoid fabricating an event or crowd.
- Prefer neutral natural lighting and realistic proportions. Keep original brand geometry and colors where those signs exist in the reference. Respect image source terms and place necessary credits in the compact references section.
- Use distinct versioned cover filenames when replacing images, regenerate immutable social card URLs, and verify both article and homepage asset references. Keep earlier social assets available for existing posts.
