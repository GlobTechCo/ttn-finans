Nvidia Faces First Earnings Without Jensen Huang as Trump Backs Possible Return

Nvidia is preparing to report quarterly results under interim leadership for the first time since co-founder Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive. The earnings release, scheduled for Tuesday, November 17, now carries an additional question for investors: whether the company can maintain its strategy and execution while its board searches for a permanent leader.

On Monday, U.S. President Donald Trump said he would not mind seeing Huang return to the role. His comments added political attention to an issue that remains a corporate governance decision for Nvidia's board. The company has not announced a change to its interim leadership or confirmed that Huang will return.

What Nvidia has formally announced

Nvidia said on November 12 that Huang had resigned as president, chief executive and a member of the board, effective immediately. The company appointed Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress as interim CEO while it searches for a permanent successor. It also named Harvey C. Jones as the board's first chair.

The resignation and interim appointment are documented in Nvidia's announcement and its filing with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission. Those disclosures establish the leadership change. Reports about the personal circumstances behind Huang's departure, including allegations involving a Chinese national suspected of espionage, are separate claims and should not be presented as proven findings.

The transition is significant because Huang has been closely associated with Nvidia's strategy for more than three decades. He helped turn the company from a graphics-chip supplier into a central provider of accelerated computing systems for artificial intelligence. Investors will want to know whether product priorities, customer relationships and long-term plans remain unchanged.

Trump supports the idea of a return

Speaking to reporters on Monday, Trump said he would not mind Huang being reinstated and said the former CEO had learned a lesson. Reuters reported that Huang was considering a comeback and had discussed the possibility with confidants, citing a Financial Times report.

Trump's remarks are not an instruction to Nvidia and do not determine who will lead the company. The board remains responsible for the appointment process. A possible return is therefore a developing scenario, not an announced corporate decision.

The comments also underline the political dimension of Nvidia's position. The company sits at the center of U.S. debates over advanced chips, export controls and competition with China. The allegations surrounding Huang's departure have intensified scrutiny of those issues, but investors still need to distinguish confirmed corporate disclosures from claims that have not been independently established.

Earnings expectations remain high

Nvidia is scheduled to release its third-quarter fiscal 2027 results at approximately 1:20 p.m. Pacific Time on November 17, followed by a conference call at 2 p.m. Pacific, or 5 p.m. Eastern. Reuters reported consensus expectations of about $2.47 in adjusted earnings per share and $109.08 billion in revenue. Those figures are analyst estimates, not company guidance or reported results.

The market will focus on data-center revenue, margins, supply availability and demand from large cloud and technology customers. Management's outlook may be more important than the headline figures. When expectations are high, even strong growth can disappoint if forward guidance suggests that customer spending is slowing or margins are under pressure.

Investors will also assess whether the leadership transition changes the company's ability to coordinate a complex product roadmap. Nvidia's next-generation Vera Rubin platform, supply-chain readiness and customer deployment schedules are important to expectations for future growth.

The broader AI business remains the central issue

The leadership story arrives during a period of uncertainty for AI-related stocks. Investors are questioning whether the scale of spending on data centers, processors, networking and power infrastructure will generate adequate returns. Nvidia's results will provide a fresh indication of demand, but one quarter cannot resolve the economics of the wider AI investment cycle.

Export restrictions remain another factor. U.S. rules governing advanced semiconductor sales to China can affect Nvidia's addressable market and the products it is permitted to sell. Any policy changes or licensing decisions could influence revenue opportunities and supply-chain planning.

TTN Finance has previously examined the company's board and leadership uncertainty in its report on the reported Nvidia CEO succession debate, as well as questions around H200 access and China-related export controls.

What investors will watch

Tuesday's call should clarify how Kress and the board intend to manage the transition, whether the search for a permanent CEO is progressing and whether Nvidia's operating priorities remain intact. Financially, investors will watch demand visibility, margins, supply constraints and the next-quarter outlook.

Trump's comments have revived discussion of a possible Huang return, but the immediate test is whether Nvidia can deliver results and reassure customers and investors without relying on speculation about its next CEO.
