Include equities, foreign exchange and crypto alongside Tech & AI when significant current events justify coverage. Do not impose a quota for any beat or substitute a stale item to fill a package.
- Publish separately reviewed articles sequentially when practical. Set each initial publication timestamp at its actual release, preserve that instant thereafter and use dateModified for substantive updates. Never manufacture intervals, backdate a story or vary timestamps merely to make simultaneous publication look organic.

## Reader-focused journalism review, October 22

Use Reuters standards (https://reutersagency.com/about/standards-values/) and AP sourcing principles (https://www.ap.org/about/news-values-and-principles/telling-the-story/) as references for verification, attribution, balance and clear distinctions between fact, allegation and analysis. Follow Google’s people-first guidance and contextual link practices (https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable): useful original reporting and reader understanding take priority over clickbait, keyword stuffing or a fixed link count. Review the lead, stakes for the reader, specific comparisons, source transparency, informative subheads and story-specific imagery before publication. Distribute useful internal links through the text rather than reserving them for a closing paragraph or Related reading box. Audience growth is an objective, not a promised outcome.

## Broad discovery and direct primary sources, October 22

- Begin each commissioned search with a broad scan across the requested sections and time window. Compare a varied pool of candidate events before selecting the strongest stories. Do not stop discovery simply because the requested number of publishable topics has been found, and do not use the first three convenient results as the default package.
- Search both major international reporting (for example Bloomberg, Reuters, The Economist and financial/business publications) and specialist/local-market reporting relevant to the event. These examples, including Watcher.Guru, are non-exclusive. A provider list, search tool, plugin or aggregator feed must not define the limits of editorial coverage. Continue discovery through other available search routes and primary sources when one route is incomplete.
- Inspect official sources proactively for news discovery, not only after a media article supplies a topic. Read relevant company newsrooms, investor-relations releases, earnings materials, securities filings, product announcements and public regulatory notices. Apply this to finance, technology and AI as well as crypto; do not concentrate only on companies already covered by TTN.
- For stablecoins, directly check issuer announcements and relevant reserve/attestation, redemption, product and regulatory disclosures. Tether/USDT and Circle/USDC are examples, not the complete universe. Distinguish the issuer, the token, the issuing entity and the jurisdictions or products involved.
- For blockchain ecosystems, directly inspect relevant foundation/developer announcements, technical blogs, upgrade documentation, governance proposals, official code releases and network-status notices. Include Bitcoin, Ethereum, XRP Ledger and Solana when relevant, while actively considering other networks, layer-2 systems, DeFi protocols, exchanges, custody, payments, tokenization and security developments. Do not treat Bitcoin as a company, a foundation as the sole voice of a decentralized network, or Ripple and XRP Ledger as interchangeable sources. Choose primary sources appropriate to the specific claim, including client developers and network governance where relevant.
- Read underlying materials and distinguish proposals, announced schedules, actual activation, measured results, incidents and resolved incidents. Record original publication/event times and timezones; a later syndicated article or edited post does not renew the event's age. Use regulators, court records, filings, independent technical evidence and additional reporting to check important claims. A company's public announcement is an attributed source, not independent confirmation of its commercial or performance claims.
- Keep a concise internal candidate record with event, section, original time, primary source, verification status and selection/rejection reason. Check whether a stronger development was missed, whether the selected stories unnecessarily repeat one company/theme and whether significant primary-source announcements were overlooked. Do not send the owner research logs, prompts or extra files unless requested.
- Apply this process whenever performing editorial research. Do not claim continuous/background monitoring or an active recurring delivery merely because this policy names sources; operational automation must be inspected and verified separately before making such a claim.
