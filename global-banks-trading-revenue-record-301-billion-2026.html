Global Banks’ Trading Revenue Set for a Record $301 Billion as Volatility Fuels Equities

Banks are projected to earn a record $301.1 billion in 2026 as volatility lifts equities and prime brokerage.

TTN Research DeskNov 24, 2026 · 8:13 AM EST

Global banks are on course to generate a record $301.1 billion in markets revenue this year, as sharp swings in equities and persistent geopolitical uncertainty drive trading activity. The forecast would put the industry above its previous annual high, recorded during the global financial crisis in 2009. But the scale of the expected increase also raises a question for investors: how much of this year’s performance can banks sustain if market volatility begins to fade? Figures from Crisil Coalition Greenwich, reported by Financial News on November 24, put global markets revenue at $301.1 billion for 2026, 14% above the previous year and higher than the former record of $286.9 billion. Banks generated $233.8 billion during the first three quarters, a 16% increase from the same period in 2025. The estimates cover more than 250 banks and include revenue from trading activities across major asset classes.

Equities have been the strongest source of growth. Industry-wide equity trading revenue is expected to increase 32% to $128.7 billion this year. Prime services revenue, which includes financing and services for hedge funds and other institutional trading clients, is forecast to rise 41% to $50 billion. By comparison, revenue from fixed income, currencies and commodities, commonly grouped as FICC, is projected to grow only 3.3% to $172.4 billion.

The difference between the two businesses reflects the unusual market environment of 2026. Artificial intelligence has become a major source of volatility in listed technology shares. Rapid changes in expectations for chip demand, data-centre investment and the commercial returns from AI have produced strong rallies as well as abrupt sell-offs. Those movements have encouraged investors to rebalance portfolios, hedge exposure and increase trading activity.

For banks, higher volumes can create more opportunities to earn from executing client orders, making markets and financing positions. Investment banks also provide derivatives that allow clients to manage exposure to individual shares, indices, interest rates and currencies. When markets move sharply, demand for these services can rise even when investors are uncertain about the direction of prices.

Prime brokerage has benefited from the same environment. Hedge funds require financing, securities lending, clearing and other services to implement complex strategies. When trading activity increases and funds use more leverage, banks can generate additional revenue from financing and client services. The expected 41% rise in prime services revenue indicates that the benefits of market volatility have extended beyond traditional cash-equity trading.

However, the FICC outlook is considerably less uniform. The category remains the larger revenue pool, with an estimated $172.4 billion in 2026, but its projected growth is modest. Revenue from G10 rates trading is expected to decline 9.5% to $36 billion. Changes in expectations for central-bank policy have created opportunities in some markets, but they have also made positioning difficult. A trading desk can benefit from higher client activity without necessarily generating stronger returns from every product.

Geopolitical shocks have added another source of business. The war involving Iran and wider international uncertainty have encouraged companies, asset managers and financial institutions to hedge currency, commodity and interest-rate exposure. Such activity can increase transaction volumes, but it is not a stable source of earnings. A sudden reduction in tensions or a clearer monetary-policy outlook could reduce the need for frequent portfolio adjustments.

The revenue forecast should also be distinguished from the profitability of individual banks. Markets revenue is not the same as net income, and a record for the industry does not mean every institution has benefited equally. Trading businesses require capital, technology, risk management and highly paid specialist staff. Higher activity can increase revenue while also raising operating costs and the potential for losses when positions move against a bank.

Competition is another pressure. Electronic trading firms such as Jane Street and Citadel Securities have expanded their presence in financial markets, challenging traditional banks in areas where speed, pricing and technology are critical. Banks retain advantages in financing, balance-sheet capacity and broad client relationships, but they cannot assume that higher volumes will automatically translate into greater market share.

The outlook for 2027 is therefore more complicated than the record forecast for this year suggests. Coalition Greenwich expects a moderation in activity, with global FICC revenue likely to decline marginally as commodity-market conditions normalise. Equities trading may also face a tougher comparison if AI-related volatility becomes less intense. The largest banks have already reported slower quarter-on-quarter growth in markets revenue through September, although executives have played down concerns about a sharp reversal.

For investors, the key issue is the quality and durability of trading income. A strong year can support bank earnings, improve returns on capital and provide a useful offset when lending or investment-banking activity is weaker. But markets businesses remain sensitive to volatility, client positioning and risk appetite. If the record is driven partly by exceptional events, it should not automatically be treated as a new baseline for valuation.

The $301.1 billion forecast demonstrates how valuable market-making and institutional trading can become during periods of uncertainty. It also illustrates why the same conditions that lift bank revenue can make future performance harder to predict. The record may be within reach in 2026, but sustaining it will depend on whether banks can maintain client activity after the current wave of market disruption passes.
