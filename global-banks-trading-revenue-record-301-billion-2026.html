Global Banks’ $301 Billion Trading Boom Faces a Tougher Test in 2027

Record markets revenue is forecast, but uneven trading growth raises questions about banks’ earnings durability into 2027.

TTN Research DeskNov 24, 2026 · 8:13 AM EST

Global banks’ markets businesses are heading for a record year, but the source of the boom is uneven and its durability is already in question. Crisil Coalition Greenwich estimates that revenue across more than 250 banks will reach $301.1 billion in 2026, 14% above 2025 and higher than the previous peak of $286.9 billion in 2009. The figure captures an exceptional year for trading desks; it does not establish that banks have become structurally more profitable.

Financial News reported on November 24 that banks generated $233.8 billion in markets revenue during the first three quarters, up 16% year over year. For the full year, equities revenue is forecast to rise 32% to $128.7 billion. Fixed income, currencies and commodities, or FICC, is expected to grow just 3.3% to $172.4 billion. Those two categories together make up the $301.1 billion estimate.

Prime services is not a third category to add to equities and FICC. It is part of the equities franchise. Revenue from prime services, which includes financing, securities lending and other services for hedge funds, is forecast to climb 41% to $50 billion. That figure helps explain the strength of equities, but adding it to the $128.7 billion equities estimate would double-count revenue.

The equity surge reflects repeated changes in investor expectations for technology companies and the capital spending behind artificial intelligence. Shifts in views on chip demand, data-centre construction and commercial returns have produced sharp rallies and reversals. Those moves create work for cash-equity desks, derivatives teams and prime brokers as clients rebalance portfolios, hedge concentrated positions and adjust leverage. Banks can earn from client activity without correctly predicting market direction.

A market-maker earns spreads for providing liquidity and managing inventory; a derivatives desk structures transactions around client exposures; a prime broker provides financing and operational services. Volatility can increase demand for all three. But a bank’s return still depends on pricing, hedging costs, balance-sheet usage and risk control. More trades do not translate automatically into a proportional increase in profit.

The prime-services forecast points to activity beyond conventional share dealing. Hedge funds generate financing and securities-lending revenue for banks when they build long and short positions, borrow stock or use leverage. The projected 41% increase suggests that funds are using a wider set of services. It can deepen client relationships, but also exposes banks to counterparty and collateral risks if market stress forces funds to unwind positions quickly.

FICC remains the larger revenue pool, but its modest growth masks differences between products. G10 rates revenue is expected to fall 9.5% to $36 billion even as the wider FICC category rises. Rates desks are sensitive to central-bank expectations, yield curves and client positioning. Uncertainty can prompt hedging, but it can also leave banks exposed to crowded trades or reduce opportunities when policy expectations become clearer.

Geopolitical disruption has supported trading and hedging. Financial News linked the 2026 environment to the war involving Iran and wider political uncertainty, which can prompt companies and investors to manage currency, commodity and interest-rate exposures. That demand is not a dependable earnings engine. If tensions ease, commodity markets normalise or monetary policy becomes easier to anticipate, some of the urgency behind those transactions may disappear.

The industry-wide record says little about which banks captured the most value. Individual results depend on franchise strength, product mix and market share. Large banks combine trading with financing and corporate relationships, while electronic firms such as Jane Street and Citadel Securities compete on execution speed and pricing. Trading businesses also consume capital, technology budgets and compensation. Revenue growth matters to investors only if it converts into sustainable earnings and returns on capital.

The 2027 outlook is more cautious. Coalition Greenwich expects activity to moderate, with FICC revenue potentially declining marginally as commodity conditions normalise. Financial News also reported slower quarter-on-quarter growth in markets revenue at major banks through September, although executives were not signalling an abrupt reversal. That is not proof that the cycle has turned, but it makes extrapolating 2026’s growth rate difficult.

For investors, the key question is how much of the record can survive a less volatile market and how much converts into durable returns. The 2026 performance demonstrates the value of scale, market-making infrastructure and institutional relationships during disruption. It also shows why a trading windfall should not automatically become an earnings baseline. Banks best positioned for 2027 will preserve client activity, control risk and defend margins as conditions change.
