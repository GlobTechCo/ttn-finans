Investors Add $64.5 Billion to Stock Funds While Pulling Money From Cash

BofA’s weekly figures show buying across equities and bonds. The reporting window ends Wednesday, so the data cannot establish how investors responded to later market swings.

TTN Research DeskOct 16, 2026 · 1:08 PM EDT

Investors added $64.5 billion to stock funds and $11.8 billion to bond funds in the week through Wednesday, according to BofA Global Research figures reported by Reuters on Friday. Money-market funds recorded $53.1 billion of withdrawals. The combination shows demand for both corporate ownership and debt rather than a single, uniform bet on the economy.

The bank’s round-up uses EPFR fund data. Within equities, US stock funds attracted $39.1 billion, while technology funds received $10.7 billion. Treasury funds took in $3.6 billion, extending their run of weekly inflows to 16 weeks. These categories overlap: Treasury funds sit within bonds, and technology exposure can sit within US equities. Adding every number together would double-count parts of the picture.

A weekly snapshot with an important cutoff

The observation period matters as much as the headline total. A report published Friday can describe subscriptions made before Thursday’s trading. It cannot demonstrate that investors bought in response to a subsequent selloff or changed their view after a later economic release.

Fund subscriptions also measure something different from investment returns. A fund can attract money while its holdings lose value. Conversely, an increase in assets under management may reflect rising prices rather than fresh subscriptions. The flow figures answer a question about money entering and leaving the monitored vehicles, not the profit earned by their owners.

Why stocks and bonds can attract buyers together

For a portfolio, equity exposure and bond exposure serve different purposes. Shares provide participation in business profits, with the risk that those profits or valuations disappoint. Bonds provide contractual payments subject to the issuer’s ability to pay. The SEC’s investor guidance also identifies interest-rate, inflation and liquidity risks; a bond fund is not interchangeable with cash.

One plausible interpretation is that buyers are balancing exposure to growth with income-producing assets. The aggregate totals cannot identify that motive. Different investors can buy different assets for unrelated reasons, so simultaneous inflows do not prove that the same portfolios made a coordinated shift.

The next evidence to watch

A useful follow-up will be whether subsequent weekly reports show sustained subscriptions after the latest volatility. Equity breadth, credit conditions and company results can then help assess whether the flows accompany a broader improvement in confidence.

TTN’s report on AI investment and borrowing costs examines the financing pressures behind part of the debate. This week’s fund figures add a positioning snapshot, but they do not settle whether that investment will earn adequate returns or where interest rates go next.
