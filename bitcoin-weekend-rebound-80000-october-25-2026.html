Bitcoin returns above $80,000 after a weekend slide as geopolitical risk meets ETF outflows

Bitcoin recovers from a 24-hour low near $79,300. Middle East concerns and two days of US ETF outflows frame the market ahead of the Fed meeting.

TTN Research Desk2026-10-25

Bitcoin was back above $80,000 on Sunday after a weekend drop below that level, as traders weighed heightened Middle East concerns against a market already facing withdrawals from US spot Bitcoin funds. The recovery regained a widely watched round number, but left the cryptocurrency close enough to the weekend low for another change in risk appetite to matter.

CoinGecko’s market page, checked on October 25, showed Bitcoin at about $80,500 and a rolling 24-hour range of roughly $79,263 to $80,949. That put the quoted price about 1.6% above the bottom of the range, while still below its upper end. These are a provider’s market snapshots, not a fixed closing price or a promise that the recovery will hold.

The latest position also differs from Saturday’s selloff report. CoinDesk recorded Bitcoin near $79,700 at 11:11 a.m. Eastern Time on October 24, down about 2.2% over the preceding day. That percentage belongs to Saturday’s reporting window and should not be carried forward as Sunday’s current 24-hour change.

Geopolitical headlines met an already cautious market

The Wall Street Journal reported that the United States was evacuating personnel from Middle East embassies and military bases after intelligence warnings about possible Iranian attacks. Its accessible article excerpt attributed the warning to US officials and described a Friday intelligence memo. TTN has not reviewed that memo independently.

CoinDesk linked Saturday’s crypto decline to the same reporting. The timing makes geopolitical concern a credible part of the market backdrop, but does not establish that every sale came from that concern. Prices combine many decisions, including discretionary selling, portfolio adjustments and transactions triggered by earlier positions.

That distinction helps avoid treating a market explanation as a measured causal result. The observable event is the price decline and partial recovery. The reported evacuation is a separate news development that could change investors’ willingness to hold volatile assets. No verified evidence in the reviewed material assigns it a precise share of Bitcoin’s move.

ETF demand weakened before the weekend

There was also a clearly dated change in a major institutional channel. Farside Investors’ table showed net US spot Bitcoin ETF outflows of $635.6 million on Thursday, October 22, followed by $137.9 million on Friday. Together, the two sessions represented approximately $773.5 million leaving the funds on a net basis.

Those figures concern fund subscriptions and redemptions, not the dollar value of all Bitcoin sold across exchanges. They nevertheless help explain why the market entered the weekend without the same demand support those products provide during periods of net inflows. The ETF ownership structure connects securities-market investors to Bitcoin exposure through a channel with its own trading and settlement arrangements.

The table’s latest daily row was Friday. Calling those withdrawals a fresh Sunday ETF outflow would be wrong. Crypto trades through the weekend, while US-listed fund shares have a different trading schedule. The distinction matters when comparing a continuously moving token price with daily fund-flow reports.

The Fed is the next scheduled policy test

The Federal Reserve’s official calendar puts its next meeting on October 27 and 28. That is an upcoming event, not a rate decision already made. Expectations around policy can affect the appeal of holding cash and other assets, including through the channels linking interest rates to crypto markets.

A firmer price above $80,000 would show that buyers are absorbing supply at higher levels than the weekend low. It would not, by itself, establish that fund demand has reversed or geopolitical risk has faded. Those claims require their own evidence: subsequent flow data, sustained trading and further verified reporting.

For now, the market is showing a partial recovery within a narrow distance of a recent downside break. The relevant next checks are whether the price can remain above that break, what Monday’s fund activity reveals and how expectations evolve ahead of Wednesday’s policy announcement. A round number gives traders a reference point; it does not provide a fundamental floor.
