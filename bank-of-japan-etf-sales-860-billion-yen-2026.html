Bank of Japan Accelerates ETF Sales While Holding Its Policy Rate at 1.25%

Annual ETF sales will more than double from January, alongside a 1.25% policy rate. Portfolio disposal and interest-rate policy have different effects.

TTN Research Desk2026-10-30

The Bank of Japan announced Friday that it would accelerate sales of its exchange-traded-fund holdings from January 2027, introducing a separate equity-market policy change while keeping its short-term interest-rate target around 1.25%. The decision arrived during a technology-led market selloff and illustrates why an unchanged policy rate does not mean every part of monetary policy is standing still.

The new annual ETF sales pace is approximately ¥860 billion measured at book value, up from ¥330 billion. At market value, the bank estimates the new annual amount at about ¥2.46 trillion using the valuation ratio on its recent sales. The two larger figures describe the same disposal pace under different valuation measures, rather than two separate programs.

Reuters reported that Nikkei futures slipped after the announcement. The policy change was an additional development during an existing decline, not evidence that it caused every earlier loss in Japanese shares. Technology stocks were already reacting to concerns about the returns on substantial AI infrastructure spending overseas.

The valuation basis is essential to understanding the size

Measured consistently at book value, the new pace is approximately 2.6 times the previous one, calculated by dividing ¥860 billion by ¥330 billion. The bank says the estimated time required to complete the disposal falls from about 112 years to about 43 years. Those figures assume the specified pace and reference holdings, rather than a promise that sales will proceed unchanged for decades.

The official statement bases the pace on trading value in the Tokyo Stock Exchange Prime Market between January and September and the market-to-book ratio of ETFs sold during that period. This ties the calibration to market activity and the assets’ valuation. It is different from announcing an immediate market order for the entire stock of holdings.

A large seller still plans to spread transactions out

The central bank’s reference document says the new pace represents approximately 0.1% of market trading value, compared with approximately 0.05% previously. It plans to distribute sales over time and approximately in proportion to each ETF’s share of its holdings. Temporary adjustments or suspensions remain possible in response to market conditions within the annual framework.

The rate decision and asset disposal are separate tools

The board held the overnight-rate guideline at approximately 1.25% by an eight-to-one vote. Hajime Takata favored a 1.5% target. The decision to accelerate ETF sales was unanimous, according to the bank’s statement. Its annual sales pace for Japanese real-estate investment trusts remains approximately ¥5 billion at book value.

A short-term policy-rate target influences funding conditions across the financial system. Disposing of equity-fund holdings changes the central bank’s participation in a particular asset market. The instruments can move on different schedules, which is why investors need to read the balance-sheet decisions alongside the headline interest-rate vote.
