Dollar Hits One-Month Low as U.S. Midterms Reshape AI Bets

The dollar reached a one-month low while Treasury prices rose, as investors reconsidered the growth and interest-rate outlook after the U.S. vote.

TTN Research Desk2026-11-04

The dollar fell to a one-month low on November 4 after Democratic gains in U.S. congressional elections prompted investors to reassess AI investment and economic growth, Reuters reported. In an early market snapshot, the dollar index stood at 101.41 and the 10-year Treasury yield was near 4.921%.

The election has changed the political setting in which investors evaluate future policy. It has not itself changed the Federal Reserve’s interest rate or enacted a new rule for AI businesses. The immediate currency movement is therefore best understood as a repricing of expectations. Whether those expectations become lasting economic consequences depends on subsequent decisions, business activity and inflation, rather than the election result alone.

The interest-rate channel

The Federal Reserve held its policy target at 3.75% to 4% on October 28. Its statement described strong economic activity, robust capital investment and elevated inflation. Two policymakers dissented in favor of an increase. That starting point matters: a market reassessment of future growth is occurring while the central bank is still concerned about price pressures, rather than against a backdrop of an announced easing cycle.

A currency reflects the relative appeal of holding assets denominated in it. If investors lower their expected path for U.S. interest rates while expectations elsewhere change less, the prospective income advantage of dollar assets can diminish. Exchange rates can adjust before any central bank acts. This is why the current policy rate and the expected future rate path should be treated as separate pieces of information.

Our guide to interest-rate differentials explains this mechanism. Hedging costs, positioning and demand for liquidity also influence exchange rates.

The same distinction applies to government bonds. A Treasury’s market yield can fall because its price rises, without the Federal Reserve changing its policy target. Investors may purchase longer-term debt when they expect weaker growth, lower inflation or greater demand for safe assets. The move in a benchmark yield is therefore evidence of changing market prices, not proof that a specific monetary decision has already been made.

AI spending reaches beyond technology shares

Investment in computing infrastructure can affect construction, electrical equipment, financing and services as well as semiconductor revenue. If investors anticipate delays or lower spending, they may reconsider activity across those connected businesses. The currency implication is indirect: a changed assessment of investment can alter expectations for growth and interest rates, which can then alter the relative attraction of dollar assets.

There is also a difference between a company’s operating prospects and the price investors pay for its stock. A business can continue increasing revenue while its valuation falls if investors demand a higher return or become less confident about later earnings. TTN’s coverage of AI investment and central-bank interest rates examines why infrastructure spending and financial conditions need to be considered together.

Political control is one input

Congressional control can influence spending negotiations, hearings and the legislative agenda, but election results do not reveal the final contents of legislation. Regulatory agencies, courts and state or local authorities have separate roles. For a data-center proposal, land use and electricity connections can matter alongside federal policy. Treating every possible obstacle as a single nationwide ban would overstate what an election outcome establishes.

For American households and businesses, a weaker dollar has uneven effects. Foreign-currency expenses become more costly when translated into dollars, while overseas income can translate into more dollars. Import prices may adjust with a delay because contracts, hedging and margins absorb part of the movement. These are general transmission mechanisms; a single trading session does not establish the size of the eventual effect on any particular bill or company.

The next test is whether inflation and investment data support the initial repricing. Persistent price pressure could keep rates elevated even if political uncertainty slows some projects.
