HSBC launches RedCoin payments, keeping the stablecoin inside its own network

The Hong Kong dollar token is rolling out to PayMe users. Its private network, reserve disclosures and lack of deposit protection define what customers receive.

TTN Research Desk2026-10-23

HSBC began launching its Hong Kong dollar stablecoin, RedCoin, through the PayMe payments app on October 23, moving the product beyond September’s naming announcement into retail use. The rollout gives an established bank a route to distribute digital tokens through an existing payments network, rather than relying first on a cryptocurrency exchange.

The bank says more than 3.3 million PayMe users will be able to activate a stablecoin wallet over the coming days. That is the size of the potential user base, not a count of active RedCoin holders. Payments can go to other users and to more than 100,000 merchants through PayMe, according to the launch announcement.

The token’s limits matter as much as its reach

RedCoin, identified as RCHKD, is referenced to the Hong Kong dollar and is redeemable one-for-one subject to its terms. It is not a U.S. dollar token. A stable value against HKD therefore does not mean a fixed value against every currency a customer might use, and a familiar bank brand does not make all digital balances the same financial product.

HSBC’s October 23 white paper describes a private, permissioned blockchain. The token uses ERC-20-compatible smart contracts, a familiar format in digital-asset software. Technical compatibility does not mean RedCoin is circulating on a public blockchain or can automatically be sent to an outside crypto wallet.

Customers also do not receive a conventional interest-bearing deposit. The white paper says RedCoin pays no interest and is not protected by Hong Kong’s Deposit Protection Scheme. That is a central distinction from the tokenized bank deposit launched by SoFi this week. Similar-looking digital dollar products can create different rights for the person holding them.

Reserve information is available, but attestations come later

The bank’s product disclosure page, dated October 23, reports approximately HK$80.58 million in token par value outstanding and HK$81.58 million in reserve assets. The amounts are issuer-reported figures for that date. They are not an independently verified transaction-volume measure, and they do not establish how many individuals used the token.

The same page says the first independent reserve-attestation report is expected after the fourth quarter of 2026. Daily published figures and an outside attestation serve different purposes: the first offers a current issuer statement; the second provides external assurance over specified evidence and dates. A future commitment to publish a report is not an already completed audit.

The white paper describes reserve assets held under a trust arrangement and a custodian holding customer tokens in an omnibus wallet, with internal records identifying each holder’s entitlement. Those arrangements explain where control and recordkeeping sit. They do not make custody, redemption and operational risk disappear simply because the underlying ledger is a blockchain.

Payments now, investment functions later

HSBC plans to add wallet functionality in its Hong Kong mobile banking app and subscriptions to tokenized investment products later this year. The product page marks those functions as coming soon. It also says customers will not be able to transfer RedCoin between the PayMe and mobile-banking-app wallets. The future expansion should therefore not be presented as one unrestricted wallet system already available today.

For merchants, the initial proposition is distribution through a payment method they may already accept. For the bank, that avoids starting with an empty network of potential counterparties. The commercial question is whether token balances lead to repeated payments and useful new services, rather than merely initial wallet activations or transfers prompted by launch promotions.

Our stablecoin explainer distinguishes a target value from a guarantee against every form of loss. RedCoin’s launch provides a concrete example of another distinction: a bank-issued token can use blockchain technology while remaining tightly controlled within the bank’s distribution channels. Usage, redemption performance and the first independent reserve reports will offer better evidence of its progress than the potential customer count alone.
