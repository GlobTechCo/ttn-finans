Binance Says Withdrawal Flows Are Stabilising After $3.64 Billion Exit Rush

Binance cited $3.64 billion in withdrawals after the breach, while outside estimates were higher. Different measurement periods complicate the recovery picture.

TTN Research Desk2026-11-02

Binance said customer withdrawals were stabilising after a reported $3.64 billion left the exchange following its security breach, according to Reuters on November 2. The outflows amounted to approximately 2.1% of assets held on the platform. The figures offer an early indication of customer behaviour after withdrawals resumed, not evidence that the investigation or asset recovery is complete.

The scale of the underlying incident remains extraordinary. Binance has said approximately $6.3 billion in cryptocurrency was stolen. The company reported that it had replaced or covered about 95% of affected assets, with less liquid tokens accounting for the remaining portion. These are company statements about its recovery programme, not an independent certification that every customer’s exposure has been resolved.

Outflows are a measure of confidence, not a verdict

When a cryptocurrency platform suffers a breach, customers face an immediate decision about custody. Some may withdraw all assets, others may reduce exposure, and many may leave funds where they are while monitoring the situation. The amount withdrawn can offer a signal of changing trust, but it cannot by itself establish whether a platform is solvent or whether its security weaknesses have been fully addressed. See also our earlier report on the restoration of Binance withdrawals.

Binance said it had around $171 billion in assets under management as of October 28 and 335 million registered users worldwide. Reuters reported the exchange’s claim that deposit volumes remained substantial and that the initial withdrawal spike was easing. These details suggest activity continued after service was restored, but the precise interpretation depends on how deposits, withdrawals and transfers between affiliated or external wallets are classified.

The distinction is particularly relevant because different data sources do not show identical numbers. Reuters reported that the crypto analytics platform DefiLlama subsequently showed withdrawals of about $5.81 billion, up from an earlier $4.36 billion reading. Binance’s $3.64 billion figure and the third-party estimate should not be presented as directly equivalent without checking their definitions and measurement periods. The company did not immediately answer a Reuters request concerning the updated external figure.

Recovery efforts and remaining exposure

Binance has said it used its own resources to purchase replacement cryptocurrency, covering about $6 billion of the reported stolen assets. The remaining portion includes tokens that may be more difficult to acquire in sufficient quantities without sharply affecting their market prices. This adds an operational dimension to recovery: reimbursement is not necessarily identical to locating and returning the original stolen coins.

The exchange reopened withdrawals in phases, prioritising tokens based on user demand. The initial group included major assets such as bitcoin and ether, as well as selected stablecoins and dogecoin. A phased approach can make technical and liquidity management easier, but customers holding unsupported or less liquid assets may face a different experience from users who hold the largest cryptocurrencies.

Broader market consequences

The incident has coincided with pressure across digital asset markets. Reuters reported that Bitcoin had recovered modestly from a six-week low and traded above $78,000 on Monday, while Ether was around $2,287 after also weakening late last week. Those are observations from the report’s publication period, not live quotes that readers should assume remain current.

Market responses extended beyond the two largest cryptocurrencies. Reuters cited data showing combined market capitalisation for the largest dollar stablecoins, USDT and USDC, had decreased by about $1.2 billion since Friday. It also reported that spot bitcoin exchange-traded funds had recorded roughly $643 million in withdrawals on Friday. These figures describe concurrent stress; they do not prove the Binance event was the sole cause of every flow.

What would show a durable recovery?

Stabilising withdrawals is a meaningful near-term signal, but trust is harder to measure than a single day’s wallet movements. A durable recovery would require consistent services, timely completion of user compensation, credible security explanations and transparent reporting of reserve and liquidity conditions.
