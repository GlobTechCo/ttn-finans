Payward xStocks: More Than 200 UK and EU Stocks Added, but Geographic Restrictions Remain

Payward has added more than 200 UK and EU stocks to xStocks, expanding its tokenized-equity range while geographic investor restrictions remain.

TTN Research DeskNov 14, 2026

Payward has expanded its xStocks tokenized-equities framework beyond U.S. listings by adding more than 200 stocks listed in the United Kingdom and European Union. The launch, announced on November 11, is the platform’s largest expansion into international equity markets so far and takes the total number of tokenized assets in the framework above 1,500.

The new listings cover some of the most actively traded companies in the UK and EU. Payward said the first batch was introduced through its partnership with financial technology company GTN, which provides global execution and custody infrastructure for the traditional assets backing the tokens.

The expansion gives eligible customers access to tokenized representations of a wider range of European companies through Kraken and supporting platforms in the xStocks Alliance. However, the addition of UK-listed shares does not mean that investors based in the United Kingdom can trade the tokens. Payward said the existing distribution and eligibility restrictions remain unchanged.

More than 200 new listings expand the framework

The tokens are available through Kraken and participating xStocks Alliance platforms. The framework operates across several blockchain networks, including Solana, Ethereum, TON, Ink and X Layer.

Payward said the expansion takes xStocks beyond 1,500 tokenized assets, including stocks, exchange-traded funds and IPO-related products. The company also reported more than $1.1 billion in assets under management, cumulative transaction volume above $45 billion and more than 550,000 unique holders worldwide.

Those figures are company-reported measures and describe the scale of the platform rather than the size of the underlying European stock markets. They nevertheless show how quickly the tokenized-equities business has expanded since xStocks initially focused on U.S. stocks and ETFs.

The UK and EU launch is also part of a broader international strategy. Payward has said that hundreds of Hong Kong-listed equities are expected to follow, with additional markets planned as the partnership and infrastructure develop.

How the tokens are backed

Payward says each xStock is backed one-to-one by the corresponding underlying share. According to the company, when an xStock is issued, the underlying share is purchased and held with a regulated custodian for as long as the token remains in circulation.

The backing structure is important, but it should not be confused with direct ownership of the listed company’s shares. xStocks are issued by Backed Assets (JE) Limited and offered to eligible Kraken customers through Payward Digital Solutions Ltd. Payward states that the tokens are not registered with local securities regulators. Holding an xStock does not automatically provide the rights of a shareholder in the underlying company.

The distinction can affect shareholder voting, corporate actions, custody arrangements and the legal rights available to token holders. Investors need to review the relevant product documentation rather than assume that a token gives them the same position as a shareholder registered through a traditional securities account.

The geographic restriction remains

Although the new tokens represent shares listed in the United Kingdom and European Union, Payward said xStocks remain unavailable to investors in the United Kingdom. The products are also unavailable in the United States and to U.S. persons. The November expansion did not change those eligibility rules.

This means the launch does not provide UK-based investors with a new route to buy tokenized versions of domestic shares through xStocks. Instead, it expands the range of underlying companies available to eligible customers in the markets where the product is permitted.

The distinction illustrates a broader issue facing tokenized securities. Blockchain infrastructure can make assets easier to distribute across digital platforms, but it does not remove securities laws, licensing requirements or jurisdiction-specific restrictions.

London Stock Exchange plans add another dimension

The expansion follows Payward’s earlier partnership with the London Stock Exchange to advance tokenization initiatives. Payward said the agreement supports plans to tokenize leading London-listed equities, while the exchange has indicated that xStocks are intended to be listed on its LSE 24 venue in 2027, subject to regulatory approval.

That proposal would represent a different development from simply adding tokens to a crypto trading platform. A listing on an exchange-operated venue would connect tokenized products more directly with established market infrastructure, although the final structure and regulatory approvals will determine how the service operates.

The broader tokenization trend is also developing across clearing, settlement and securities administration. For related coverage, see the report on the NYSE’s tokenization initiative with transfer agents and TTN Finance’s coverage of the DTCC tokenization service.

Expansion does not remove the market’s structural limits

But the expansion does not resolve the main questions surrounding tokenized securities: how closely token holders’ rights match conventional share ownership, how custody and redemption work, and which investors can legally access the products.

For now, xStocks has widened the range of European companies available onchain without opening the product to investors in the UK or United States. The next stage will depend not only on adding more listings and blockchain networks, but also on regulatory approval, custody arrangements and the ability to make tokenized securities accessible across different jurisdictions.
