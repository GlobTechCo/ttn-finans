BitGo Launches Global Markets as Crypto Custody Expands Into Financing and Hedging

The new business combines trading, financing, collateral management and derivatives after BitGo’s earlier purchase of NYDIG’s institutional trading operation.

TTN Research Desk2026-11-04

BitGo launched an expanded Global Markets business on November 4, combining institutional trading with financing, derivatives, collateral management and settlement capabilities. The move extends the company’s commercial pitch beyond safeguarding digital assets: it wants to help institutions finance positions and manage their market exposure as well. Pete Janney, who joined from NYDIG, will lead the business.

The launch builds on a transaction completed earlier in the year. BitGo’s SEC-filed announcement dated August 27 said it had acquired NYDIG’s institutional trading business and related assets. Approximately 30 employees joined, along with institutional client relationships. Wednesday’s development is the broader Global Markets offering, rather than a newly announced purchase of the whole of NYDIG.

That distinction helps explain the strategy. A custody relationship can establish where assets are held, but it does not necessarily provide every service an institution needs to use them. Bringing execution, financing and risk-management expertise into the same commercial offering could deepen those relationships. Whether clients adopt the expanded services, and on what terms, remains the business test.

Financing turns asset holdings into collateral

BitGo says the new business includes lending against digital-asset collateral, margin monitoring, collateral calls and liquidation management. It also offers spot and privately negotiated block execution, alongside options, swaps and structured solutions for eligible counterparties. These are different activities with different exposures, even when a single institution uses several of them.

Consider an illustrative institution that owns digital assets but needs cash for another commitment. Borrowing against those holdings can provide liquidity without an immediate sale. The position is no longer simply an unleveraged investment, however. A decline in collateral value can create a demand for additional assets or repayment, depending on the financing contract.

Our explanation of crypto margin and liquidation examines why a financed position can be closed before the investor has lost every dollar of its initial margin. Custody arrangements and lending terms address separate questions. A custodian can protect the holding of an asset without protecting its market price or preventing a contractual collateral call.

Settlement is part of the service, not just the final step

BitGo’s existing Go Network documentation describes settlement within its qualified-custody infrastructure. For a bilateral settlement, one party requests an asset in exchange for another, and the transfers take place together once the required approvals are complete. That coordination is intended to reduce the exposure created when one side delivers before receiving the agreed payment.

The off-exchange settlement service has a different workflow. According to BitGo’s Allocate documentation, supported assets can remain in custody while balances are allocated to participating trading venues. Orders are placed at the partner venue rather than through that allocation interface. Settlement occurs on a predetermined schedule, which depends on the partner and is generally at least daily.

The same documentation makes clear that the treatment is not universal. Some assets traded at a partner venue may fall outside the supported off-exchange settlement setup and may be held by the exchange instead. A broad claim that every transaction automatically retains the same custody protection would therefore obscure a material operational distinction.

These details matter because execution speed, custody and final settlement are not the same service. Our coverage of Hong Kong’s tokenized interbank settlement project examines a separate infrastructure effort. Across different systems, the commercial question is similar: how participants establish that both the asset transfer and the corresponding payment have been completed.

Derivatives add choices and obligations

An institution may use an option or swap to change its exposure without selling the underlying holding. That can serve a hedging objective, but the result depends on the instrument and the wider portfolio. A derivative can also introduce counterparty exposure, collateral requirements or a payoff that behaves differently from an outright asset purchase.

The launch announcement describes these products as available to eligible counterparties. It does not establish identical access across jurisdictions, a standard price for financing or a guaranteed investment return. Those details belong to the relevant entity, product documentation and client agreement. The expanded menu should not be interpreted as a retail banking offer.

For clients, combining services may simplify some operational coordination while increasing the importance of understanding the roles performed by each legal entity. Safekeeping, arranging a trade, extending credit and managing a fund create different relationships. A shared brand does not make the obligations in those relationships interchangeable.

Integration must produce measurable client demand

For BitGo, the strategic opportunity is to earn a larger role in an institution’s activity after assets arrive on its platform. Its August acquisition announcement explicitly linked the transaction to greater client engagement and a more integrated service. Those were management’s expected benefits. The November launch alone does not establish additional assets, trading volume or revenue.

The useful evidence will be adoption of the new services, the quality of execution and financing through changing market conditions, and the economics disclosed in subsequent reporting. Broadening custody into capital-markets services can create additional revenue opportunities. It also requires the company and its customers to manage credit, liquidity and operational demands that a simple buy-and-hold relationship does not fully capture.
