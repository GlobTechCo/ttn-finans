Binance Reopens Bitcoin Withdrawals After $6.3B Hack

Withdrawals are reopening in phases after the $6.3 billion theft. Binance says it has covered 95% of affected assets, with some tokens still outstanding.

TTN Research Desk2026-11-01

Binance began reopening cryptocurrency withdrawals on November 1 after a reported $6.3 billion security breach, according to company updates. The exchange said it had restored backing for most affected customer assets with its own resources, but restoring access and replacing balances are not the same as recovering the stolen funds. Withdrawals were scheduled to resume in phases across several networks.

Binance’s November 1 notice scheduled an initial withdrawal phase for 06:00–09:00 UTC and listed Bitcoin, Ethereum, BNB Smart Chain, Solana, XRP and other networks. AFP subsequently reported that Bitcoin was the first network reopened. Availability remained dependent on asset-specific checks.

A major breach followed by a controlled reopening

The disruption began on October 29 at approximately 20:05 UTC, according to a company incident update. Binance said attackers had compromised its hot wallets, leading to unauthorized withdrawals of assets valued at approximately $6.3 billion. The preliminary findings evolved as the investigation progressed: the exchange initially cited a potentially compromised laptop, but later said it had identified additional points of breach. It also said it had found no evidence that wallet private keys had leaked. Those are company statements rather than independently established final forensic conclusions.

The suspension left customers unable to move assets from the exchange during the interruption. It became a test of the operating model of a large centralized exchange, in which users depend on the platform's infrastructure to move assets that may ultimately settle on decentralized networks. The Bitcoin network itself did not need to be compromised for customers to lose the ability to withdraw their Bitcoin from an affected intermediary.

What does the 95% recovery figure mean?

Binance said in its November 1 update that it had replenished backing for approximately 95% of the affected amount using corporate resources and acquiring replacement tokens; that figure does not mean 95% of stolen assets had been recovered from the attackers. The firm said its largest affected balances, including BTC, ETH, USDT, BNB, SOL and USDC, were fully backed. It described the remaining roughly 5% as less-liquid assets that would take longer to acquire at the necessary scale.

Why Bitcoin reopened first

Binance said withdrawal networks and assets would come online progressively and that customers could experience delays or failed requests during the initial rollout. It directed users to individual network status and withdrawal pages for current availability. Users should rely on those operational notices rather than assuming that one announcement means all services have returned to normal.

The incident has also reignited scrutiny of reserve disclosures. Binance said it would provide proof-of-reserves information, but an exchange reserve snapshot alone does not necessarily establish the full range of corporate liabilities or explain the financial treatment of every loss. An independent assessment would need to consider custody controls, off-chain obligations, reserve composition and the separation of customer and corporate funds.

The questions that remain

Investors and customers will be watching for three developments: the complete restoration of withdrawal routes, independent confirmation of balances and replacement reserves, and a detailed post-incident account explaining the attack path and the safeguards introduced afterward. Binance said it intended to publish further findings when the investigation was complete.
