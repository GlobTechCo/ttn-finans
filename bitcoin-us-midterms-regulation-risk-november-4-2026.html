Bitcoin Recovers Toward $78,000 After Post-Election Six-Week Low

A rebound toward $78,070 followed a six-week low. Prospects for U.S. crypto legislation leave exchange-custody risks unresolved.

TTN Research Desk2026-11-04

Bitcoin briefly reached a six-week low on November 4 before recovering to about $78,070 at 07:15 GMT, according to Reuters. Ether traded around $2,292. The agency linked the initial weakness to uncertainty about the U.S. legislative outlook after Democratic congressional victories, alongside continuing concern about exchange custody. The rebound matters as much as the dip: the early trading pattern does not support describing an uninterrupted post-election collapse.

For the digital-asset industry, the immediate issue is the difference between favorable political expectations and an enforceable operating framework. An election can change the perceived probability of future legislation. It does not, by itself, alter the Bitcoin protocol, rewrite an exchange’s customer agreement or resolve the legal treatment of every token. Those separate questions are frequently compressed into a single judgment about whether an outcome is positive or negative for crypto.

Political expectations have a market price

When traders expect easier access, clearer rules or greater institutional participation, they may incorporate those expectations into asset prices before the underlying changes occur. If that anticipated path becomes less certain, the premium can shrink. Such a move does not require a new restriction to have taken effect. The change is in the range of possible future outcomes and the probability investors attach to each.

Regulation also affects different parts of the industry differently. A payment-token issuer, a securities intermediary and an offshore derivatives venue do not have identical businesses or obligations. A rule that benefits one service might increase costs for another. Bitcoin’s price is therefore an imperfect summary of the operating outlook for every company described as a crypto business.

TTN’s earlier reporting on the industry’s involvement in the U.S. midterms provides context for why the election attracted market attention. The relevant next steps are concrete: proposed legislative language, committee action, agency decisions and implementation dates. Campaign commitments and changes in the composition of Congress are inputs into that process, rather than substitutes for completed policy.

Custody risk is a different exposure

The distinction between an asset and the institution holding it is particularly important during a security incident. A customer may have economic exposure to Bitcoin while relying on an exchange to maintain records and process withdrawals. The market price of the token and the customer’s practical ability to retrieve it are separate risks. A stable quotation cannot establish that a custodial platform is operating normally.

The distinction also matters for Bitcoin exchange-traded products: owning a fund share and controlling Bitcoin directly involve different custody arrangements and legal claims.

The macroeconomic backdrop still matters

The Federal Reserve left its target rate at 3.75% to 4% on October 28 and said inflation remained elevated. That is relevant background for assets whose valuation depends heavily on investors’ willingness to bear risk. Interest-bearing alternatives create a comparison for capital allocation, even though they have different liquidity, credit and market characteristics from digital tokens.

Leverage can amplify that adjustment. A position financed with borrowed funds may need additional collateral when prices move against it. Forced reductions can then add selling pressure. This is a general mechanism, not a claim that a verified quantity of liquidations caused the November 4 move. Without reliable position and transaction data, assigning a precise share of a price decline to forced selling would be speculative.

The session leaves three separate questions: the next steps in legislation, the reliability of custody arrangements and the strength of investor demand. A partial price recovery resolves none of them.
