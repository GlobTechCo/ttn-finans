How this calculator works

This tool models a long position with a fixed quantity. Investment amount is initial margin when leverage exceeds 1x. Fees are deducted separately from P&L and do not reduce the modeled quantity or increase the return denominator. The model assumes the position survives until the chosen exit price.

Entry notional = initial margin × leverage
Quantity = entry notional ÷ entry price
Exit notional = quantity × exit price
Gross P&L = exit notional − entry notional
Entry fee = entry notional × entry fee rate ÷ 100
Exit fee = exit notional × exit fee rate ÷ 100
Net P&L = gross P&L − entry fee − exit fee
Return on initial margin = net P&L ÷ initial margin × 100

The field labeled current price is the assumed exit price. Both fee rates are entered by you; no exchange tariff is supplied. The tool excludes funding payments, borrowing costs, slippage, taxes, liquidation and short positions. A leveraged position may be liquidated before reaching the displayed exit price, so a theoretical loss can exceed margin. At zero initial margin, dollar amounts are zero and percentage return is undefined.

A worked trade with fees

With $1,000 initial margin, 2x leverage, a $60,000 entry price and $65,000 exit price, entry notional is $2,000 and quantity is 1/30 of a coin. Exit notional is $2,166.6667. At illustrative entry and exit fees of 0.1% each, gross profit is $166.6667, entry fee is $2 and exit fee is $2.1667. Net profit is $162.50 and return on initial margin is 16.25%. These are user-defined hypothetical rates, not current exchange prices.

At an unchanged $60,000 exit price with the same margin, leverage and fee rates, gross P&L is zero, both fees are $2, net P&L is −$4 and return is −0.4%. Setting both fee rates to zero restores the result before costs.

Common questions

Does this calculate a liquidation price?

No. Liquidation depends on the exchange’s maintenance margin, fees and collateral rules, which this calculator does not model.

Can I use it for a short position?

No. This tool models a long position: price increases generate gains and price decreases generate losses.
