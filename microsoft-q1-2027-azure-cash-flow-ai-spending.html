Microsoft’s Azure Growth and Cash Flow Lift Its AI Investment Case

Azure revenue rose 48% and free cash flow reached $18.6 billion. Delayed infrastructure deliveries mean Microsoft’s spending test is still ahead.

TTN Research Desk2026-10-29

Microsoft reported 48% growth in Azure revenue and $18.6 billion in free cash flow for its September quarter, giving investors a stronger financial case for its expensive artificial-intelligence expansion. The results, released Wednesday, also contained a timing complication: some infrastructure deliveries slipped, pushing part of the spending burden into later periods rather than eliminating it.

That combination makes the quarter more informative than a simple verdict that AI spending has paid off. Microsoft is selling more cloud capacity and generating cash after substantial investment. But the cash available in one period depends partly on when equipment arrives and payments fall due. The company's earnings call made clear that demand and the associated investment requirements remain large.

The spending bill has moved, not disappeared

Chief Financial Officer Amy Hood said capital expenditures totaled $45.2 billion, below the roughly $50 billion Microsoft had previously anticipated, because some third-party capacity deliveries were delayed. On the earnings call, she projected more than $50 billion of capital expenditure in the December quarter and an acceleration in the second half of the fiscal year, including the deferred capacity. These are management forecasts, not completed purchases.

The capital-expenditure headline and the cash-flow calculation also measure different things. Microsoft reported $58 billion in operating cash flow and approximately $39.4 billion of cash paid for property and equipment, leaving about $18.6 billion in free cash flow. Capital spending also incorporates finance-lease accounting and timing effects. Subtracting the broader $45.2 billion figure from operating cash flow would therefore produce a different measure.

This distinction is central to comparing AI infrastructure companies. Signing for capacity, bringing a data center into service and paying for it do not necessarily happen in the same quarter. Our guide to free cash flow explains why the definition and payment schedule matter alongside the resulting number. Deferred deliveries can improve near-term cash retention while limiting the capacity available to earn revenue.

Cloud demand is strong, but it is not all AI revenue

Microsoft's total revenue increased 19% to $92.3 billion in the first quarter of fiscal 2027, which ended September 30. Its Microsoft Cloud measure reached $63.9 billion, up 30%. Those sales span multiple businesses; Azure's growth rate should not be applied to the entire cloud total, nor should that total be presented as a standalone measure of AI sales.

The company's investor metrics define Azure to include cloud and AI consumption-based services and virtual desktops. They also show Microsoft Cloud's gross margin at 66%, compared with 68% a year earlier. Strong sales growth can thus coexist with a lower percentage margin when the mix of services changes. Scaling revenue and improving the economics of each workload are related, but separate, tests.

For the December quarter, Hood forecast Azure growth of 49% to 50% in constant currency, meaning with exchange-rate effects removed. The forecast assumes continued efficiency gains while demand remains above available supply. TTN's analysis of inference economics examines why utilization and computing cost influence what a provider can earn from that demand.

Backlog is future work, not cash already collected

Commercial remaining performance obligations reached $677 billion. This measure represents contracted revenue still to be recognized; it is not the same as quarterly revenue or available cash. Although it increased substantially from a year earlier, the published metrics put it slightly below the preceding quarter's $678 billion. The comparison illustrates how the same order book can look different depending on the period selected.

Reuters reported that free cash flow exceeded the Visible Alpha consensus by roughly $10 billion and Microsoft shares rose more than 3% in after-hours trading. That was an initial market response, not a completed Thursday trading session. The next evidence will come as delayed capacity is delivered: how much additional revenue it enables, what customers consume, and how much cash remains after the larger investment bill is paid.
